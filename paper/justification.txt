Bivariate-bicycle codes underpin IBM's planned fault-tolerant architecture, but their single-ancilla syndrome circuits lose distance unless X and Z checks are interleaved, and how short a distance-preserving circuit can be was open. We settle this for translation-invariant circuits: the minimum period preserving full distance is 10 for the gross code and 11 for [[90,8,10]], and we determine the best distance at every shorter period. The impossibility results and the full-distance circuits are backed by checked proof certificates. The mechanism, an integer cochain of round offsets whose negative cycles block light logicals, turns a qualitative design heuristic into a decidable criterion applicable beyond these two codes.
