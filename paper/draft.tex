\title{Interleaving recovers the full circuit distance of bivariate-bicycle codes}

\author{Guancheng Pan}
\email{3250101086@zju.edu.cn}
\affiliation{Zhejiang University, Hangzhou, China}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Every non-interleaved single-ancilla syndrome-extraction circuit has circuit
distance at most the distance of an extended code, which can fall below the code
distance: at most 11 for the gross code $\code{144,12,12}$ and, as we show, at most 8
for $\code{90,8,10}$. We show that interleaving recovers the full code distance. We
construct single-ancilla, interleaved circuits with circuit distance 12 for the gross
code, answering an open question of Strikis, Browne and Beverland, and 10 for
$\code{90,8,10}$, two above every non-interleaved circuit, and prove both under
circuit-level depolarizing noise for every number of rounds. The method fixes an
extended code and turns circuit design into a finite decision problem over its
lightest logicals, solved exactly and certified with DRAT proofs; a direct search on
the detector error model confirms both results. The circuits have periods of 18 and
16 time steps per round, against 8 for the IBM circuits.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bivariate-bicycle (BB) codes~\cite{Bravyi2024} are leading candidates for
low-overhead quantum memory; IBM's architecture is built on the gross code, with
parameters $\code{144,12,12}$~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}. What a syndrome circuit
delivers is its circuit distance $\dcirc$, the minimum number of circuit faults that
flip a logical observable without triggering any detector; at low physical error
rates it governs the logical error rate.

No circuit for the gross code had been shown to reach $\dcirc=d=12$; the IBM
circuit, with 8 time steps per round, has $\dcirc\le10$~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}.
Strikis, Browne and Beverland (SBB)~\cite{Strikis2026} analysed single-ancilla
circuits, in which each check is measured by one dedicated ancilla coupled by CNOTs.
They append to the check matrix the ``residual'' errors that an ancilla fault
spreads onto the data, obtaining an extended code of distance $\dext$, and show that
$\dcirc\ge\dext$ for every such circuit (their Prop.~3), while $\dcirc\le\dext$ when
the circuit is \emph{non-interleaved} in their sense, i.e.\ when all $X$-type CNOTs
precede all overlapping $Z$-type CNOTs or vice versa (their Sec.~II\,A and Cor.~1).
Since every CNOT order gives $\dext\le11$ for the gross code, non-interleaved circuits
are capped at 11 (their Prop.~4); their depth-8 circuit (their Fig.~12, hereafter
``Fig.~12'') was conjectured to reach it. They left open ``whether any interleaved
circuit can achieve circuit distance 12''~\cite{Strikis2026}. For the $\code{90,8,10}$
code of the same family the best known value is 8: the IBM circuit has
$\dcirc\le8$~\cite{Bravyi2024}, proven exact by MaxSAT~\cite{Tesseract2025}.

We answer this question in the affirmative and show that the gap between Prop.~3 and
Cor.~1 can be wider than one. For the gross code we construct a single-ancilla,
CNOT-based, interleaved circuit and prove $\dcirc=12$ for every number of rounds
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main})\cl{41,40}. For $\code{90,8,10}$ every choice of CNOT orders
gives $\dext\le8$, so every non-interleaved circuit has $\dcirc\le8$; we construct an
interleaved circuit with $\dcirc=10=d$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:bb90})\cl{53}. The idea
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}) is to fix an extended code and choose CNOT orders and times
such that none of its lightest logicals can be realised by undetected circuit faults.
This turns circuit design into a finite decision problem that we solve exactly and
certify, a tool for single-ancilla circuits in general. Our computation of $\dext$
also proves SBB's conjecture that Fig.~12 has $\dcirc=11$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:fig12})\cl{10}. The new circuits run with periods of 18 and 16
time steps per round instead of 8\cl{44,53}; we discuss this cost at the end.

Exact circuit distances are known for hypergraph-product codes~\cite{ManesClaes}, for
the smallest BB circuit~\cite{Webster2026}, and as certificates for other qLDPC
constructions~\cite{Liang2026,SunCai2026}. Shaw and Terhal's morphing circuit with
the same qubit count acts on a $\code{144,12,12}$ code that they call ``a second
`gross' code'', with $\dcirc\le12$~\cite{ShawTerhal2025}; a morphing idling circuit for
the gross code is ``believed to have circuit distance 12''~\cite{Wills2026}. We note
that this end-cycle code is not equivalent to the gross code, since its minimum
stabiliser weight is 9 rather than 6\cl{2}. For $\code{90,8,10}$ the upper bound for
the unchanged IBM circuit returns to 10 under infinitely $Z$-biased ancilla
noise~\cite{BiChangPuri2026}; we keep depolarizing noise, change the circuit, and
prove the value\cl{53}. Using SBB's lower bound via non-interleaved counterparts to
screen interleaved schedules was proposed in Ref.~\cite{Nguyen2026}; for the gross
code that bound gives at most 11. Check-dependent CNOT orders are an established
tool~\cite{TomitaSvore,KishonyFowler,Strikis2026}\cl{40}.

\paragraph{Setting.}
We consider memory experiments in the $Z$ or $X$ basis: data initialisation, $R$
rounds of syndrome extraction and transversal readout. Each check has one ancilla
that is reset, coupled by six CNOTs and measured once per round; rounds of different
checks may overlap in time (pipelining). Faults are the single-operation Pauli
components of standard circuit-level depolarizing noise (SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}).
Faults with identical detector and observable effects are merged into one
detector-error-model (DEM) mechanism~\cite{Gidney2021stim}, and $\dcirc$ counts
distinct mechanisms. For a check with CNOT order $(q_1,\dots,q_6)$, an ancilla fault
after the $k$-th CNOT deposits the residual $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$ on the data; the
extended code appends all residual columns $(Hs,Ls)$ to the data columns ($H$: check
matrix, $L$: logical operators). Every single fault, propagated to the readout, has a
data image that is zero or one extended-code column (SBB, Table~I; proved for our
template in SM~\ref{sm:reduction}~\cite{SM}). For Fig.~12, $\dext=11$ in both bases, with
exactly 4224 weight-11 extended logicals per basis, all realisable in Fig.~12 by
eleven undetected faults\cl{11,13}.

\paragraph{Key idea.}
Interleaving decouples $\dcirc$ from $\dext$ in one direction only: $\dcirc\ge\dext$
still holds, but $\dcirc$ can exceed it. We say that an extended logical \emph{lifts}
if one can choose, for each of its columns, a circuit fault with that image such that
the chosen faults trigger no detector; a lift is an undetected logical error of the
same weight. Reaching $\dcirc=d$ requires an extended code of the largest possible
distance and a timing under which none of its lightest logicals lifts. The first
requirement is met by an invariance: up to the check itself, residuals of length 5,
4, 3 and 2 equal $\{q_1\}$, $\{q_1,q_2\}$, the complement of $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$ and
$\{q_5,q_6\}$, so the extended code depends only on these three unordered sets per
check. Reversal and the swaps $s_{12}$, $s_{56}$ of positions 1,2 and 5,6 preserve
them; all eight orders they generate share the extended code of Fig.~12, its distance
11 and its 4224 weight-11 logicals (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}a)\cl{32,42}.

The second requirement concerns time. Reversing an order keeps the extended code but
changes which fault realises each residual column and when its spread reaches the
neighbouring checks (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}b). In Fig.~12 a hook column $\{q_5,q_6\}$
is produced after the fourth CNOT and lands after the neighbouring $Z$ checks have
read those qubits, so every data error can be placed after its reads. After
reversal, the same column comes only from a fault between the second and third CNOT,
whose four-qubit spread reaches one neighbour after its readout and another before
it; along a cycle of checks, each seeing two errors of the logical, the early and
late constraints leave one check with odd parity, so a detector fires. For all 360
logicals of this ``ten data plus one hook'' form, the lift fails at $R=1$, and every
irreducible conflicting set of detectors (one whose constraints alone forbid the
lift, but no proper subset does) has between 4 and 10 detectors; the smallest such set
has 4, 5 or 9 detectors\cl{49}. With the Fig.~12 orders themselves blocking is
impossible: every retiming family of Table~\ref{tab:fig12} is obstructed (End
Matter)\cl{16}.

\paragraph{Construction.}
We searched gross-code schedules symmetric under the 24 colour-preserving
translations, choosing for each of the six (type, colour) classes one of the eight
orders and its CNOT times with a SAT solver, under legality, determinism and the
blocking of every weight-11 extended logical in a bulk Pauli-frame model
(SM~\ref{sm:search}); candidates were then certified exactly. The result
(Table~\ref{tab:circuit}; timing diagram in SM~\ref{sm:circuits}) measures the gross
code itself, is legal, deterministic, pipelined and interleaved, and every check's
order differs from that of Fig.~12\cl{41,42}. A second period-18 circuit and a
period-21 circuit in which all ancillas share one frame have the same
distance\cl{31,43}. For $\code{90,8,10}$ the same kind of search over fully
translation-invariant schedules gave the circuit in the lower part of
Table~\ref{tab:circuit}, in which every data qubit alternates $X$- and $Z$-type CNOTs
six times per period\cl{53}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under SD6 noise, the gross-code circuit of Table~\ref{tab:circuit} has $\dcirc=12$ in
both memory bases for every number of rounds $R\ge1$.
\end{theorem}

The proof rests on two lemmas valid for every circuit of our template.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction]\label{lem:reduction}
An undetected fault set of size $w$ that flips a logical observable yields an
extended-code logical of weight at most $w$, formed by its nonzero fault images.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Add one observable per row of $H$ and $L$ on the final data readout. The
observable of row $H_i$ equals the sum of all detectors of check $i$, which telescope
from the first measurement to the final readout. For an undetected fault set the
$H$ parts of the images therefore sum to zero and the $L$ parts to the logical effect;
each image is zero or one column, and equal columns cancel. $\square$\cl{33}

\begin{lemma}[Light logicals]\label{lem:structure}
An undetected logical of weight $\dext$ consists of $\dext$ faults whose images are
distinct columns forming an extended logical. One of weight $\dext+1$ consists either
of $\dext+1$ such faults, or of $\dext$ such faults forming an extended logical
together with one fault of zero image.
\end{lemma}
The proof is a two-line count (End Matter)\cl{53}. Deciding $\dcirc\ge\dext+2$ is thus
a finite problem: decide which extended logicals of weight $\dext$, with or without one
zero-image fault, and of weight $\dext+1$ lift.

For the gross circuit, Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} with $\dext=11$ excludes weight
$\le10$, and only weight 11 remains.

\begin{lemma}[Finite rounds]\label{lem:lift}
For $R=1,\dots,32$ and both bases, none of the 4224 weight-11 extended logicals of the
gross circuit lifts.
\end{lemma}
We verified this by an exact computation, a CP-SAT model per logical that chooses one
DEM mechanism per column subject to every detector sum vanishing, with two independent
implementations; the same program finds a lift for all 4224 logicals in Fig.~12
(positive control)\cl{42}. Each infeasible instance was also encoded as CNF; batches
combined by a selector disjunction were refuted with DRAT proofs verified with
drat-trim, whose LRAT cores were verified with cake\_lpr (SM~\ref{sm:lift})\cl{51}. The
inputs, $\dext=11$ and the list of 4224 logicals, are confirmed by two independent
exhaustive searches rather than by proof certificates\cl{11,13}.

\begin{lemma}[All rounds]\label{lem:gap}
For the gross circuit, a weight-11 undetected logical for some $R$ implies one for
some $R'\le32$.
\end{lemma}
A fault-free window of 52 layers would split such a logical into two undetected
parts, one of weight $\le10$; deleting fault-free periods then bounds the number of
rounds (End Matter)\cl{43}. A weight-12 data logical, applied as flips after
initialisation, triggers no detector and flips the observable, which completes the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}\cl{42}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fig12}
The Fig.~12 circuit of SBB has $\dcirc=11$ in both bases for every $R$, as they
conjectured.
\end{corollary}
This is SBB's Prop.~3 and Cor.~1 combined with our value $\dext=11$\cl{10,11}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Gap of two for $\code{90,8,10}$.}
For every assignment of per-check CNOT orders, the extended code of the $X$-check side
contains a logical of weight at most 8, as we verified with a SAT encoding whose
unsatisfiability is certified by a DRAT proof checked with drat-trim
(SM~\ref{sm:bb90}). Hence $\dext\le8=d-2$ for every single-ancilla circuit of this
code, and every non-interleaved circuit has $\dcirc\le8$ by SBB's Cor.~1, a value that
is attained\cl{53}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:bb90}
Under SD6 noise, the $\code{90,8,10}$ circuit of Table~\ref{tab:circuit} has
$\dcirc=10$ in both memory bases for every number of rounds $R\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof sketch.} Its orders give $\dext=8$, with exactly 585 extended logicals of
weight 8 and 16830 of weight 9 per basis. By Lemmas~\ref{lem:reduction}
and~\ref{lem:structure}, $\dcirc\ge10$ holds if none of these lifts, the weight-8 ones
also together with one zero-image fault. For $R=1,\dots,12$ and both bases none does,
as found by three independent implementations. Rounds are separated in time, which
reduces every $R$ to $R\le12$, and a weight-10 data logical gives the upper bound (End
Matter)\cl{53}.

To our knowledge, the two circuits of this work are the first explicit
single-ancilla circuits whose circuit distance exceeds the extended-code distance of
every choice of CNOT orders\cl{53}. Both lower bounds were also checked without the
extended code, by an exact branch-and-bound search on the full DEM (an independent
implementation of the same family of methods as SM~\ref{sm:dext}): for the gross
circuit there is no undetected logical of weight $\le11$ at $R=1$, 2, 3; for the
$\code{90,8,10}$ circuit none of weight $\le9$ for $R\le12$, which with round
splitting proves Theorem~\ref{thm:bb90} without extended-code input
(SM~\ref{sm:direct})\cl{54}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Optimality of the gross period.}
In a bulk Pauli-frame model of lifting, no gross-code schedule symmetric under the 24
colour-preserving translations with orders in the class of Fig.~12 has period below
18, or below 21 if all ancillas share one frame; a model lift is a genuine error for
logicals with one length-2 residual and was confirmed for all 991 sampled others (End
Matter)\cl{45}. Among all 24 colourings by cosets of translation subgroups of index 2, 3, 4, 6, 8
or 9, with one order type per colour, only the class of Fig.~12 has $\dext\ge11$\cl{46}.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
SBB's obstruction is specific to non-interleaved circuits: both codes admit
single-ancilla circuits that preserve their full distance, and for $\code{90,8,10}$
interleaving gains two units over every non-interleaved circuit. Fixing the extended
code and searching over orders and timing turns an open-ended circuit search into a
lift problem against finite lists of extended logicals of weight $\dext$ and
$\dext+1$, which is decided exactly; it applies to every single-ancilla circuit whose
distance is limited by SBB's Cor.~1.

\emph{Cost and outlook.} The gross circuit uses the qubits of the IBM and SBB
circuits but 18 time steps per round instead of 8, with 3168 idle locations per round
instead of 288 and 2.8 times the total fault probability\cl{44,50}. Under the same SD6
noise and identical BP-OSD and Relay-BP decoders, it is worse than the IBM circuit at
every sampled $p\ge10^{-3}$ (about $1.5\times10^3$ times at $p=2\times10^{-3}$, at least
3 times at $10^{-3}$), while at matched fault mass the circuits perform alike\cl{50}.
Rescaling only the idle channel, $p_{\rm idle}=\lambda p$, shows where the structure
pays off: at $\lambda=0$ the period-18 circuit beats Fig.~12 at $p=4\times10^{-3}$ (ratio
0.37 [0.28, 0.49] with BP-OSD) and is statistically indistinguishable from the IBM
circuit at $p=3$--$4\times10^{-3}$, whereas at $\lambda=0.1$ it is worse than Fig.~12 by
2.3--3 times per round (SM~\ref{sm:idle})\cl{52}. The $\code{90,8,10}$ circuit has
period 16, three steps of which contain no CNOT, against 8 for the IBM circuit; its
logical error rate has not been evaluated\cl{53}. Shorter periods are therefore the
natural next goal: can $\dcirc=12$ and $\dcirc=10$ be reached at period 8 by less
symmetric schedules? Correlated faults, leakage and CZ-native compilations are outside
the scope of this work.

\section*{End Matter}

\emph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}.} Let $w$ faults form an undetected logical,
$z$ of them with zero image and $c$ pairs with equal nonzero images, which cancel. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} the remaining distinct columns form an extended logical of
weight $w-z-2c\ge\dext$. For $w=\dext$ this forces $z=c=0$; for $w=\dext+1$ it forces
$c=0$ and $z\le1$. $\square$\cl{53}

\emph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}.} Let $D=18$ be the period. After the last fault of
a fault set at layer $a$, the data frame is constant from layer $a+D-1$ on, because
only ancillas still in flight can change it. If an undetected weight-11 logical had no
fault in a window of $G=2D+\Delta-1=52$ layers, where $\Delta=17$ is the longest
reset-to-measurement span of a check, no detector could respond to both the faults
before and after the window; both parts would be undetected, their logical effects
would add to a nonzero value, and one part would have weight $\le10$, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}. So a weight-11 logical spans at most $10\times51$ layers.
The noisy circuit is periodic: inserting an 18-layer block at any layer $x$ with
$4\le x\le18(R-1)$ (layers numbered from 0) turns the $(R-1)$-round circuit into the
$R$-round one. Deleting fault-free blocks before and after the logical leaves every
detector it touches unchanged, which maps it into a circuit with at most 32 rounds
(term-by-term count in SM~\ref{sm:gap}). With Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} no weight-11
undetected logical exists for any $R$. $\square$\cl{35,43}

\emph{Details for Theorem~\ref{thm:bb90}.} The lists of 585 weight-8 and 16830 weight-9
extended logicals were obtained by two independent enumerations (a depth-first and a
meet-in-the-middle search) with identical results. In this circuit the zero-image
mechanisms that trigger detectors are exactly the ancilla readout flips, 45 per round;
all zero-image mechanisms are admitted as candidates. The three lift implementations
(a frontier dynamic program, a CP-SAT model and a per-logical SAT model) find no lift
for $R=1,\dots,12$ in both bases; as positive controls they find 315 weight-9 lifts for
a nearby interleaved circuit with $\dcirc=9$, and 585 weight-8 lifts for a
non-interleaved circuit with the same extended code. All ancillas are reset at step 0
and measured at step 15, so rounds are separated in time and the circuit is periodic.
A minimal undetected logical with a fault-free round inside its span would split into
two undetected parts, one lighter; a weight-9 logical therefore spans at most 9
rounds, and deleting fault-free rounds after the second and before the last maps it
into a circuit with at most $2+9+1=12$ rounds. A weight-10 data logical, applied as
flips after initialisation, fires no detector and flips the observable
(SM~\ref{sm:bb90})\cl{53}.

\emph{Retimings of Fig.~12.} Among periodic schedules of period $\le8$ keeping the
Fig.~12 orders there are four conflict-free ones up to translation, all with
injection-verified weight-11 undetected errors for $R=1,\dots,6$. For longer periods,
in a bulk Pauli-frame model a data error is seen by a check at the first readout after
it, and a logical is model-liftable if its errors can be timed so that every check
sees an even number of them between consecutive readouts. A model lift of a logical
with ten data errors and one length-2 residual is a genuine undetected error once the
number of rounds is large enough (a one-dimensional Helly argument). Every legal
circuit keeping the orders has such an error in each family of Table~\ref{tab:fig12},
as shown by SAT encodings restricted to these logicals, each accompanied by a DRAT
proof (SM~\ref{sm:fig12})\cl{14,15,16,17,18}.

\emph{Optimality of the period.} In the same model, among gross-code schedules
symmetric under the 24 colour-preserving translations with orders in the class of
Fig.~12, every legal schedule with period $P\le17$ has a model-liftable weight-11
logical, and so does every schedule with $P\le20$ in which all ancillas share one
reset and measurement frame; the minimal periods are therefore 18 and 21 in this
model. These encodings use all modelled logicals; the model-to-circuit step is proven
for logicals with one length-2 residual and was confirmed on the exact detector error
model in all 991 sampled cases with several residual columns. In the same model,
invariance under a translation subgroup of order 12 already excludes $P\le11$, and any
non-trivial subgroup excludes $P=8$; schedules without symmetry are open. The order
class is exact: among the 24 colourings by cosets of translation subgroups of index 2,
3, 4, 6, 8 or 9, with one order type per colour, only the class of Fig.~12 has
$\dext\ge11$ in both bases (SM~\ref{sm:search})\cl{45,46,47,38}.

\section{Notation and noise model}\label{sm:notation}
\emph{Code.} The gross code~\cite{Bravyi2024} has $\ell=12$, $m=6$, $A=x^3+y+y^2$,
$B=y^3+x+x^2$, $H_X=[A\,|\,B]$, $H_Z=[B^{T}\,|\,A^{T}]$; qubits and checks are
indexed by $i=6a+b$ for the monomial $x^ay^b$, with left and right blocks of 72
qubits each; $n=144$, $k=12$, $d=12$. Term labels: $A_1=x^3$, $A_2=y$, $A_3=y^2$,
$B_1=y^3$, $B_2=x$, $B_3=x^2$. The $X$ check at $(x,y)$ acts on left qubit
$(x,y)+A_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)+B_k$; the $Z$ check at $(x,y)$ on left qubit
$(x,y)-B_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)-A_k$. The colour of a check is $(x+y)\bmod3$;
the 24 translations $(a,b)$ with $a+b\equiv0\pmod3$ preserve colours.

\emph{Circuit template.} Each check has one ancilla. In every round it is reset,
applies its six CNOTs in its order (ancilla as control for $X$ checks, as target for
$Z$ checks) and is measured. A schedule assigns each check a reset step, six CNOT
steps and a measurement step modulo the period $P$; windows may wrap around the
period boundary, so that rounds of different checks overlap. A $Z$-basis memory
initialises the data in $|0\rangle$, runs $R$ rounds and reads all data in the $Z$
basis; detectors are the first measurement of each $Z$ check, the comparison of
consecutive measurements of each $Z$ check, and the comparison of the last
measurement with the parity of the final readout. The $X$ basis is analogous.

\emph{Noise and $\dcirc$.} SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}: after every CNOT a
two-qubit depolarizing channel; after every reset a flip; before every
measurement a flip; on every qubit idle in a time step a single-qubit depolarizing
channel (also during reset and measurement layers). A fault is one Pauli component
of one channel. Faults with identical detector and observable signatures are one
DEM mechanism, and $\dcirc$ counts mechanisms. For lower bounds we use the CSS
relaxation: in a $Z$-basis memory only $Z$-check detectors and the $X$ part of
each fault are kept; this can only lower the minimum. Upper bounds are checked on
the full circuit. Lower bounds hold for any sub-model of SD6.

\section{Invariance of the extended code}\label{sm:invariance}\cl{32,42}
Consider a $Z$-basis memory; only $X$ errors matter. An $X$ error on the ancilla of
an $X$ check after its $k$-th CNOT propagates to the suffix $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$;
$Z$-check CNOTs (data controls, ancilla target) do not change the $X$ frame on the
data. Each fault's final data $X$ frame is therefore zero, a single data qubit, or a
proper suffix of an $X$-check order, and its column is $(H_Zs,L_Zs)$. Multiplying
by the check itself, which has zero column, a suffix of length 5 is equivalent to
$\{q_1\}$ (a data column), of length 4 to $\{q_1,q_2\}$, of length 3 to the
complement of $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, and a suffix of length 2 is $\{q_5,q_6\}$. Hence the
column set is determined by the three sets $\{q_1,q_2\}$, $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$,
$\{q_5,q_6\}$ of every check. Reversal maps these to $\{q_5,q_6\}$, the last three
and $\{q_1,q_2\}$, which produce the same columns, and $s_{12}$, $s_{56}$ leave them
unchanged. The $X$ basis is symmetric. For circuits A, B and C we also computed the
extended codes directly: both bases have 360 merged columns, and the column sets
coincide with those of Fig.~12.

\section{The extended code: $\dext=11$ and the 4224 weight-11 logicals}\label{sm:dext}\cl{11,13}
The extended code of Fig.~12 has 360 merged columns per basis (144 of check-weight
3, 144 of weight 6, 72 of weight 9). The 24 colour-preserving translations map each
check's order to the order of its image check and preserve the logical classes
(checked by solving $L\circ T=ML+NH$ with $\mathrm{rank}\,M=k$); the columns form 15
orbits.

\emph{Search.} To decide whether $Hx=0$, $Lx\neq0$ has a solution with $|x|\le W$ we
use a complete depth-first search: a minimum-weight solution may be translated so
that it contains the representative of its lowest orbit; the search branches on the
columns covering an unsatisfied check row, excluding earlier siblings, so that the
branches partition the search space; it prunes when the depth plus
$\lceil|s|/w_{\max}\rceil$ exceeds $W$ ($s$ the current syndrome, $w_{\max}$ the
largest column weight) and closes the last step by table look-up. The program was
validated against unpruned meet-in-the-middle enumeration on smaller instances and
by checking that split runs add up to unsplit runs.

\emph{Results.} At $W=10$ there is no solution in either basis
($\approx2\times10^{10}$ nodes per basis), on two independently constructed
instances, and under a second symmetry group of order 8 (45 orbits,
$\approx5\times10^{10}$ nodes per basis); weight 11 is attained. Hence $\dext=11$.
At $W=11$, two different search programs enumerate the same 4224 extended logicals
per basis, all in free orbits of the 24-element group (176 orbits); 360 of them
consist of ten data columns and one residual of length 2. Both $\dext=11$ and the
completeness of the list of 4224 weight-11 logicals rest on two independent
exhaustive searches; we did not obtain a checkable proof certificate for them (a
direct CNF encoding is beyond CDCL solvers already at weight 6).

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}}\label{sm:reduction}\cl{33}
We work in a $Z$-basis memory; the $X$ basis follows by exchanging the roles of $X$
and $Z$ and of the two check types. In a $Z$-basis memory only $X$ components
matter. For a fault we track its \emph{data $X$ frame}: the set $s$ of data qubits carrying an $X$
error after the fault has been propagated through the rest of the circuit to the
final data readout. Its \emph{image} is the column $(H_Zs,\,L_Zs)$.

\emph{Gates of the template.} An $X$-check ancilla is prepared in $|+\rangle$, is the
control of its six CNOTs (data qubits as targets) and is measured in the $X$ basis.
A $Z$-check ancilla is prepared in $|0\rangle$, is the target of its six CNOTs (data
qubits as controls) and is measured in the $Z$ basis. Under a CNOT, an $X$ on the
control is copied to the target, an $X$ on the target stays there, and $Z$ errors
never create $X$ errors. Hence: (a) an $X$ on a data qubit never leaves it (as a
target it is not copied; as a control it is copied only onto $Z$-check ancillas,
which are never controls and are measured and reset without acting back on the
data); (b) an $X$ on a $Z$-check ancilla never reaches the data; (c) an $X$ on an
$X$-check ancilla after its $k$-th CNOT of an instance is copied onto exactly the
targets $q_{k+1},\dots,q_6$ of the remaining CNOTs of that instance and is then
discarded by the next preparation (the $X$-basis measurement does not see it). These statements use
only the direction of each gate, not the relative timing of different checks, so
they hold for interleaved and pipelined schedules alike.

\emph{Single faults.} We go through the SD6 locations. In each case $s$ is empty, a
single data qubit, or a suffix $\{q_k,\dots,q_6\}$ of one $X$-check order.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Data qubits: an $X$ or $Y$ from idle noise, a flip after data preparation, or
a flip before the final readout gives $s=\{q\}$ by (a); a $Z$ gives $s=\emptyset$.
\item $Z$-check ancillas: preparation and measurement flips, idle errors and any
error on the ancilla alone give $s=\emptyset$ by (b).
\item $X$-check ancillas: the preparation flip and the measurement flip are $Z$
errors in this basis and give $s=\emptyset$; an $X$ or $Y$ from idle noise before the
first CNOT gives the full support of the check, after the $k$-th CNOT the suffix
$\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$, after the sixth CNOT $s=\emptyset$, by (c).
\item Two-qubit Pauli $P_a\otimes P_d$ after the $k$-th CNOT of an $X$ check
(ancilla $a$, data $q_k$): the $X$ part of $P_a$ gives $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$ and
the $X$ part of $P_d$ gives $\{q_k\}$, so $X_aI$ gives $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$,
$IX_d$ gives $\{q_k\}$, and $X_aX_d$ (or any combination with $X$ parts on both)
gives their union $\{q_k,q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$, the suffix starting at $q_k$.
\item Two-qubit Pauli after a CNOT of a $Z$ check (data $q$, ancilla): the data part
gives $\{q\}$ or $\emptyset$ by (a), the ancilla part gives $\emptyset$ by (b).
\end{enumerate}
\emph{Images.} A single data qubit gives a data column of the extended code. A
suffix $\{q_k,\dots,q_6\}$ with $2\le k\le6$ is a residual, i.e.\ one of the
appended columns (for $k=6$ it is the data column of $q_6$). The full support
($k=1$) is the check itself, whose image is zero because $H_Z$ and $L_Z$ commute
with the $X$ stabilisers. So every single fault has image zero or exactly one
extended-code column. Merging faults into DEM mechanisms does not change this,
since merged faults have identical effects on all detectors and observables,
including the augmented ones introduced below.

\emph{Telescoping.} Augment the circuit with one observable for each row of $H_Z$
and of $L_Z$, measured on the final data readout. For every $Z$ check $i$, the sum of
all its detectors (first measurement, all consecutive comparisons, final
comparison) telescopes to the parity of row $i$ of $H_Z$ on the final readout, i.e.\
to the augmented observable $H_i$. Hence an undetected fault set has images whose $H$
parts sum to zero, and whose $L$ parts sum to its logical effect. Its nonzero images
therefore sum to an extended-code logical; cancelling equal pairs leaves a set of
distinct columns of weight at most the number of faults. If the set has eleven
faults and $\dext=11$, the eleven images are nonzero, distinct and form one of the
listed weight-11 logicals. The template has no $X$-check detectors in a $Z$-basis
memory; adding them would only remove undetected fault sets, so the bound also holds
with all detectors.

\emph{Computed check.} For every circuit and every $R$ used in S6 we confirmed both
facts on the DEM in both bases: each mechanism's per-check detector parity equals the
$H$ part of its image, its logical effect equals the $L$ part, and the set of images
is exactly the 360 extended columns.

\section{Computation for Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}}\label{sm:lift}\cl{34,42}
By S5, a weight-11 undetected logical selects, for each of the eleven columns of
one listed extended logical, one DEM mechanism with that image, such that the
detector sums vanish. For each of the 4224 logicals per basis, a CP-SAT model has a
Boolean variable per mechanism of the eleven image groups, an exactly-one
constraint per group, and an XOR constraint per detector. Infeasibility for all 4224
logicals at a given $R$ means that no weight-11 undetected logical exists at that
$R$.

Results: circuit A, $R=1,\dots,32$, both bases: all infeasible. A second,
independently written lift program agrees and extends the range to $R=36$. Circuit
B: $R=1,\dots,24$; circuit C: $R=1,\dots,14$ and $16$, plus a solver-free
meet-in-the-middle check (split the eleven groups in two halves, enumerate all
detector sums of each half, intersect) for $R\le3$; all infeasible. Positive
control: on Fig.~12 the same programs find a realisation for all 4224 logicals.

\emph{Certificates.} For circuits A ($R\le32$), B ($R\le24$) and C ($R\le14$ and 16),
both bases and all 4224 logicals, the lift instances were encoded as CNF. Instances
were combined into batches by a selector disjunction: the batch formula is the
disjunction of its members, each guarded by its own selector variable, with no
variables shared between members, so that unsatisfiability of a batch implies that
of each member. Each batch was refuted by CaDiCaL; each refutation is a DRAT proof
verified with drat-trim, whose LRAT core was verified with the formally verified
checker cake\_lpr. As a positive control,
every one of the 4224 Fig.~12 instances at $R=1$ is satisfiable and decodes to a valid
lift\cl{51}.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}}\label{sm:gap}\cl{35,43}
Notation: period $D=18$; check $q$ has start $s_q$, instance $j$ is reset at
$s_q+jD$ and measured at $s_q+jD+\Delta_q$ with $\Delta_q\le D-1$; $\Delta=\max_q
\Delta_q=17$; $S=\max_q s_q-\min_q s_q=5$. Faults are attached to layers.

\emph{(1) Settling.} If all faults of a set $F_<$ lie at layers $\le a$, the data
frame they produce is constant from layer $a+D-1$ on: only an ancilla fault can
later change the data, through the remaining CNOTs of its own instance, all at
layers $\le a+D-2$; a freshly reset ancilla never pushes errors onto the data (an
$X$-check ancilla only picks up $Z$ errors from the data, a $Z$-check ancilla only
$X$ errors). Every instance reset after $a+D-1$ is fault-free and measures $H_ce$.
If all faults of $F_>$ lie at layers $\ge b$, measurements before $b$ are unaffected.

\emph{(2) Splitting.} Let $F=F_<\cup F_>$ be a weight-11 undetected logical with no
fault strictly between $a$ and $b$. A comparison detector $(c,j)$ responds to $F_<$
only if $s(c,j-1)<a+D-1$ and to $F_>$ only if the measurement of instance $j$, at
$s(c,j-1)+D+\Delta_c\le a+2D+\Delta-2$, is at a layer $\ge b$. A first-round
detector $(c,0)$ responds to $F_>$ only if $b\le s_c+\Delta_c\le21$, so with $a\ge0$
it responds to both parts only if $b-a\le21<G$. A final detector responds to both only if
$b-a<D+S+\Delta$. Hence for $b-a\ge G=\max(2D+\Delta-1,\,D+S+\Delta)=52$ no detector
responds to both parts; each part is undetected, their logical effects add to a
nonzero value, and one part has weight $\le10$, contradicting Lemma~1. Consecutive
faults of $F$ are therefore less than $G$ layers apart, and $F$ spans at most
$10(G-1)=510$ layers.

\emph{(3) Deletion.} The noisy SD6 circuit with $R$ rounds equals the circuit with
$R-1$ rounds with an 18-layer block inserted at layer $x$, for every
$x\in[4,18(R-1)]$ (checked layer by layer in both bases; the template is exactly
periodic). Removing a block $[x,x+D)$ that contains no fault of $F$ removes one
measurement of every ancilla; a detector of the shorter circuit that does not
straddle the cut equals a detector of the longer one, and one that straddles it
equals the sum of the two detectors around the removed measurement. $F$ therefore
stays undetected, with the same logical effect, provided the frame entering layer
$x+D$ of the longer circuit equals the frame entering layer $x$ of the shorter one;
this holds if $x+D\le f_1$ (all zero; $f_1$ the first fault layer) or
$x\ge f_{11}+2D-2$ (data frame settled by (1), all ancillas in flight fault-free).
\emph{(4) Counting.} Write $f_1\le f_{11}$ for the first and last fault layers of
$F$; by (2), $f_{11}-f_1\le10(G-1)=510$. \emph{Front:} while $f_1\ge4+D=22$, the block
$[4,22)$ is fault-free and can be deleted at $x=4$, shifting $F$ by $-18$ layers;
afterwards $f_1\le21$ and hence $f_{11}\le21+510=531$. \emph{Back:} a block can be
deleted at $x=f_{11}+2D-2$ as long as this $x$ is an admissible insertion point,
$x\le18(R-1)$. When no further deletion is possible,
$18(R-1)<f_{11}+2D-2\le531+34=565$, so $R-1\le31$ and $R\le32$. (Using $2D-1$ in
place of $2D-2$, as a more conservative variant does, gives the same bound; the
threshold $2D-3$ would already suffice.) Thus
every weight-11 undetected logical of a circuit with $R$ rounds yields one of a
circuit with at most 32 rounds, and with Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} none exists for any $R$.

For circuits that alternate with their time reverse, Shaw and Terhal reduce any
number of rounds to a single round~\cite{ShawTerhal2026}; the gap argument above
needs no such symmetry.

\emph{Circuits B and C.} For circuit C, rounds are separated in time; a fault-free
round inside the span of $F$ splits it as in (2), so $F$ occupies at most 11
consecutive rounds, and periodicity reduces any $R$ to $R\le13$. For circuit B we use
a slab version of (2): a fault-free slab from the earliest reset to the latest
measurement of one round splits $F$, provided that on every data qubit every
propagating CNOT of round $k$ inside the overlap with round $k+1$ precedes every
reading CNOT of round $k+1$. We verified this condition for all rounds of the
circuits with $R=3,8,24$ in both bases; it reduces any $R$ to $R\le23$.

\section{The $\code{90,8,10}$ code: bound and Theorem~\ref{thm:bb90}}\label{sm:bb90}\cl{53}
\emph{Code and circuit.} $\ell=15$, $m=3$, $x=S_{15}\otimes I_3$, $y=I_{15}\otimes S_3$,
$A=x^9+y+y^2$, $B=1+x^2+x^7$, $H_X=[A\,|\,B]$, $H_Z=[B^T\,|\,A^T]$~\cite{Bravyi2024};
$n=90$, $k=8$, and an exhaustive search finds no logical of weight $\le9$ and 558
$X$-type logicals of weight 10, so $d=10$. Labels: $A_1=x^9$, $A_2=y$, $A_3=y^2$,
$B_1=1$, $B_2=x^2$, $B_3=x^7$; qubits and checks are indexed by $i=3a+b$, and the check
at $(x,y)$ acts as in S1. The 45 translations are code automorphisms that preserve
the labels, and the circuit of Table~\ref{tab:circuit} is invariant under all of them.
It is legal (each data qubit takes part in at most one CNOT per step; every check's
six CNOTs cover its support at distinct steps inside its window) and deterministic
(each of the 405 overlapping pairs of $X$ and $Z$ checks shares an even number of
qubits on which the $X$ CNOT precedes the $Z$ CNOT). $X$-type CNOTs occupy steps
$\{1,3,5,6,8,10\}$ and $Z$-type CNOTs steps $\{2,4,6,7,9,12\}$, so each data qubit's
cyclic sequence is $XZXZXZ$ and no partition of a round into an $X$ part and a $Z$
part exists.

\emph{Every choice of orders has $\dext\le8$.} Up to $s_{12}$, $s_{56}$ and reversal,
a check of this code has 90 order types; for 36 of them the extended code of that
check alone already has a logical of weight $\le8$. For the remaining 54, consider
each of the 558 weight-10 $X$-type logicals $l$: any realised residual columns inside
$l$ whose total size reduction is at least 2 (one triple or quadruple, or two
disjoint pairs) give, with the rest of $l$, an extended logical of weight $\le8$. We
encode the choice of one admissible type per check as a SAT instance with one clause
for each such combination (17910 combinations, each verified explicitly as an
extended logical of weight $\le8$; 3555 variables and 21690 clauses), in a form that
only strengthens the statement (types are required ``at least one'' per check, and
realisation is a one-way implication). CaDiCaL reports unsatisfiability and the DRAT
proof is verified by drat-trim. Hence for every assignment of orders the $X$-side
extended code has a logical of weight $\le8$, and every non-interleaved circuit has
$\dcirc\le8$ by SBB's Cor.~1. A non-interleaved circuit whose extended code equals
that of Table~\ref{tab:circuit} lifts all 585 weight-8 extended logicals at $R=1$, so 8
is attained.

\emph{Lists.} The extended code of the orders of Table~\ref{tab:circuit} has 225 merged
columns in each basis (5 orbits under the 45 translations). There are no extended
logicals of weight $\le7$, exactly 585 of weight 8 and 16830 of weight 9 in each
basis; a depth-first search and an independent meet-in-the-middle enumeration give
identical sets.

\emph{Lift.} Following Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}, we decide for every weight-8 extended
logical whether it lifts, with or without one additional zero-image DEM mechanism,
and for every weight-9 extended logical whether it lifts. Zero-image mechanisms with
detectors are, in every round, exactly the 45 ancilla readout flips (checked for
$R=1,\dots,12$); all zero-image mechanisms of the DEM are admitted as candidates, so
this description is not used. For $R=1,\dots,12$ and both bases no instance lifts
(full counts, no early stopping), with three implementations: a frontier dynamic
program, a CP-SAT model, and a per-logical SAT model; at $R=1$ an exhaustive
enumeration of the products agrees. Controls: a nearby interleaved circuit with
$\dcirc=9$ gives 315 weight-9 lifts at $R=1$ and $R=2$; the non-interleaved circuit above
gives 585 weight-8 lifts, 540 weight-8 lifts with a zero-image fault, and 16830 weight-9
lifts at $R=1$. The image property (S5) and the telescoping identity were asserted on
the DEM for every $R$ used.

\emph{All $R$.} In round $j$ all CNOTs lie in layers $16j+1,\dots,16j+12$ and all
measurements in layer $16j+15$, and the noisy circuit with $R+1$ rounds equals the
$R$-round circuit with one 16-layer block inserted (checked for $R=3,\dots,14$, both
bases). If a minimal undetected logical has a fault-free round strictly inside its
span, the faults before that round form an undetected set by themselves (from that
round on every measurement reads the same syndrome, consistent with the final
readout), so both parts are undetected and one of them is a lighter logical. Hence a
weight-9 logical spans at most 9 rounds, and deleting fault-free rounds after the
second and before the last maps it into a circuit with at most $2+9+1=12$ rounds.

\emph{Upper bound.} A weight-10 data logical, applied as flips after data
initialisation, fires no detector and flips the observable for $R=1,2,3,6,10,13$ in
both bases.

\section{Direct search on the detector error model}\label{sm:direct}\cl{54}
The search looks for a minimum-weight set of DEM mechanisms with zero detector sum
and nonzero logical effect. It branches on the mechanisms covering an odd detector,
excluding earlier siblings so that the branches partition the search space; it prunes
any partial set whose detector sum and logical effect both vanish (such a set cannot
be contained in a minimal solution), bounds the remaining weight by a packing of
detectors with disjoint covering mechanisms, and closes the last level by table
look-up. Root cases are split by the orbits of the translations that preserve the
detector set and the logical classes (checked by rank conditions). This is the same
family of methods as the extended-code search of S4, implemented independently with
different pruning; its $R=1$ results were reproduced by a second, independently
written implementation, which also reproduced $\dext=11$ and the list of 4224 logicals
of S4, and both were tested on 1672 random instances with known answers. The searches
produce no proof certificates.

Results. Gross circuit A: at $R=1$ there is no undetected logical of weight $\le11$ in
either basis and one of weight 12 is found; at $R=2$ (66 root cases) and $R=3$ (96 root
cases) there is none of weight $\le11$, and the injection of S8 gives 12.
$\code{90,8,10}$ circuit: for $R=1,\dots,12$ there is none of weight $\le9$ in either
basis, and weight 10 is found for $R=1,2,3$; the interleaved control circuit gives 9.
For the $\code{90,8,10}$ circuit, take a minimal undetected logical of weight $w\le9$
at any $R$: by the splitting argument of S9 it spans at most $w$ rounds and maps into
a circuit with at most $w+3\le12$ rounds, contradicting the search. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:bb90} for every $R$ without extended-code input; it uses the
separation and periodicity checks of S9. At $R=1$ a SAT formulation of the same
question on the DEM is also unsatisfiable in both bases.

\section{Fig.~12 of SBB: $\dcirc=11$ and retimings}\label{sm:fig12}\cl{10,13,14,15,16,17,18}
\emph{$\dcirc=11$.} Fig.~12 is non-interleaved, so SBB's Prop.~3 and Cor.~1 give
$\dcirc=\dext$ for every $R$; $\dext=11$ by S4. The weight-11 extended logicals are
realised by eleven circuit faults (checked by injection for $R=1,2,3$ in both bases).

\emph{Lift structure.} All 4224 weight-11 extended logicals per basis lift to
undetected circuit errors of Fig.~12 ($R=1,2,3$).

\emph{Retimings with period $\le8$.} Consider periodic schedules with period
$P\le8$ shared by all checks, one reset, six CNOTs at distinct offsets and one
measurement per check and period, and no data qubit in two CNOTs at one step.
Weight 6 forces $P=8$ and all offsets; keeping the Fig.~12 orders leaves one start
per check. A backtracking search without symmetry assumptions finds exactly 32
conflict-free assignments, 4 up to global translation (two SAT encodings agree); all
four are colour-uniform, deterministic and non-interleaved, and one is Fig.~12 itself.
For each of them and every $R=1,\dots,6$ in both bases we injected a weight-11 error
that fires no detector and flips the observable.

\emph{Longer periods (bulk).} For longer periods we use a Pauli-frame model of the
bulk. A data $X$ error at time $\theta$ on qubit $w$ is first seen by $Z$ check $c$
(readouts at $T_c+nP$) in instance $H_c=\min\{n:T_c+nP>\theta\}$, i.e.\ for
$\theta\in(T_c+(H_c-1)P,\,T_c+H_cP)$. A weight-11 logical is \emph{model-liftable}
if instance indices $H_c$ can be chosen such that the errors seen by each check can
be paired, both errors of a pair being seen in the same instance (so all comparison
detectors cancel), and, for every
data error, the intervals of its three checks have a common point. For three open
intervals of length $P$ on the line, pairwise intersection, which is a difference
constraint $H_c-H_d\le\lfloor(T_d-T_c)/P\rfloor+1$, implies a common point
(one-dimensional Helly); an SD6 fault on $w$ is available there (the two-qubit channel
after the CNOT with the latest left endpoint contains $X_w\otimes I$). A residual of
length 2 is deposited by an ancilla fault after the fourth CNOT of its check, which
fixes its arrival times. \emph{Direction used.} If the difference constraints of a
logical of ten data errors and one length-2 residual are feasible, the corresponding
eleven faults, placed away from the time boundaries, form a genuine undetected
logical error; this was derived independently twice and confirmed by 75 direct
injections, each firing no detector and flipping the observable. Non-existence
statements need exactly this direction: an unsatisfiable search for a schedule in
which no logical is model-liftable shows that every schedule has a model-liftable
weight-11 logical, and when the search uses only logicals of the proven form, every
schedule has a genuine weight-11 undetected error for sufficiently many rounds. The converse (model
blocking implies exact blocking) is not used and fails at the initial time boundary,
where the exact lift can succeed via first-round detectors. Blocking a model-liftable logical requires a negative cycle
in its constraint graph; we encode this for all modelled logicals, for CP-SAT and in
a lazy-clause SAT loop. The certificates block every model-liftable logical.
Re-running the same encoders with only the 360 logicals per basis of the form ten
data columns plus one length-2 residual gives UNSAT for every row of
Table~\ref{tab:fig12}, each accompanied by a DRAT proof, so each obstruction rests only
on the direction proven above\cl{16}. The resulting status for retimings that keep the Fig.~12 orders is given
in Table~\ref{tab:fig12}. Per-check-free pipelined schedules with $P\ge9$ and
different CNOT orders are not covered; the latter is how the new circuits escape.

\section{Search and optimality}\label{sm:search}\cl{38,39,45,46,47}
\emph{Search.} The circuits were found with SAT models over schedules symmetric
under the 24 colour-preserving translations. Variables: for each of the six
classes, one of the eight orders of the Fig.~12 class, a start and six CNOT times;
constraints: legality (six distinct times modulo $P$ per data qubit), determinism
(on every overlapping pair of $X$ and $Z$ checks, the number of shared qubits on
which the $X$ instance acts before the $Z$ instance is even, for every instance
offset), and, added lazily, one clause per frame-model-liftable weight-11 extended
logical forbidding the timing that makes it liftable. Models were then certified
with S5--S7. With the Fig.~12 orders pinned the model is unsatisfiable, in line
with S9. As a control, 233 random legal deterministic schedules of the
common-frame family, sampled without the blocking clauses, all have liftable
weight-11 logicals.

\emph{Minimum period.} With the same model and no circuit fixed, pipelined schedules
with $P=8,\dots,17$ and common-frame schedules with $P=20$ are unsatisfiable. Every
unsatisfiable clause set in the data set (129 in total: 104 for the obstruction and
optimality instances with all logicals, and 25 length-2-only instances for
Table~\ref{tab:fig12}) is accompanied by a DRAT proof produced by CaDiCaL and
verified with drat-trim; the LRAT core was additionally verified with
cake\_lpr (SM~\ref{sm:data})\cl{51}. For the
pipelined case a second, independent encoding (absolute times, one-sided difference
literals) agrees for $P=8,\dots,17$. The final clause sets were re-solved from scratch
by independent SAT solvers (three for $P=20$ common frame and $P=12$, two for
$P=17$). Completeness of the family encoding: at $P=8$ it enumerates exactly the 944
legal deterministic circuits found by an independent backtracking search, and 75
independently sampled legal deterministic circuits at $P=12$, 17 (pipelined) and 20
(common frame) are all models. Soundness of the clauses: in 991 sampled cases,
including logicals with several residual columns, every lift asserted by the frame
model was confirmed on the exact DEM, and in 99 sampled cases every violated clause
corresponded to an exact lift. Unsatisfiability means that every legal deterministic
circuit of the family has a model-liftable weight-11 logical. For logicals of ten data
errors and one length-2 residual this implies a genuine weight-11 undetected error for
sufficiently many rounds (S9); for logicals with several residual columns the same
implication is confirmed in the sampled cases above but not proven in general. The
minimum periods are therefore established within the model and refer to
sufficiently many rounds. Restricting the CNOT window of class $X_0$
or of class $Z_2$ to at most 14 steps is unsatisfiable at $P=24$, while each of the
other four classes can be so restricted; since a window spans at most $P-3$ steps,
this suggests why $P\ge18$ is needed, but the bound itself rests on the direct runs.
At $P=8$ all 944 legal deterministic circuits of the family were also checked
directly: each has a weight-11 undetected error at $R=1$ in the $Z$ basis.

\emph{Uniqueness of the order class.} Per check, the extended-code columns are the
data columns and three residual sets; up to reversal there are 90 types per basis, of
which 45 contain a residual of length 3 that, together with nine data columns, forms
a weight-10 extended logical. For each of the 24 colourings by cosets of translation
subgroups of index 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9 (3, 4, 3, 12, 1 and 1 subgroups), with one type
per colour, a SAT search with witness clauses leaves only the translates of Fig.~12
with $\dext\ge11$ in both bases; every witness used was re-verified as at most ten
distinct columns of that assignment's extended code with vanishing $H$ part and
nonzero $L$ part. Colourings of index 12, 18, 24 and 36 and per-check-free
assignments are not covered.

\emph{Less symmetry.} In the same model, pipelined schedules invariant under the
order-12 subgroup generated by $(1,2)$ are obstructed for every $P\le11$, and
schedules invariant under any subgroup of order $\ge2$ are obstructed at $P=8$. Schedules without
symmetry, and the remaining subgroups at $P=12,\dots,17$, are open.

\section{Sensitivity to idle noise}\label{sm:idle}\cl{52}

by $\lambda$, $p_{\rm idle}=\lambda p$; $\lambda=1$ reproduces SD6 exactly and
$\lambda=0$ coincides with circuit-level depolarizing noise without idle errors (both
checked on the detector error models). The CX-native variant uses SI1000
coefficients with CX, RX and MX treated as native operations; it is not the standard
SI1000 model. Compiling CX to $H\,{\rm CZ}\,H$ and using Z-basis resets and
measurements would raise the per-round fault mass of the period-18 circuit by about
12\% and that of the period-8 circuits by 6--9\%, so the variant slightly favours the
period-18 circuit.

\emph{Per-layer rates.} Dividing per-round rates by the period treats every layer as
having unit duration, consistent with SD6 but not with physical time. Each round of
the period-18 circuit has 12 CX-only layers and 6 layers with measurements or resets;
Fig.~12 has 2 and 6. If measurement and reset layers are much longer than CX layers,
the ratio of round durations falls from 2.25 towards 1.1--1.3, and the per-layer
advantage of the longer period largely disappears.

\emph{Leading order.} For an ideal decoder at low $p$, the per-round rate is dominated
by weight-12 logical errors with total weight $A_6p^6$. Summing over the enumerated
families (weight-11 logicals and one type of weight-12 logicals), the ratio

\section{Verification and data}\label{sm:data}
All circuits were built and simulated with Stim~\cite{Gidney2021stim}; exact
feasibility questions were decided with CP-SAT and with CDCL SAT solvers, and the
extended-code searches use a dedicated depth-first search. The following were
obtained with two independent implementations that agree: the construction of the
extended code and $\dext=11$; the list of 4224 weight-11 logicals; the lift
computation of Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}; the enumeration of period-8 schedules; and the
minimum-period runs. The arguments of Lemmas~\ref{lem:reduction} and \ref{lem:gap}
were checked independently, and their structural premises (template identity,
image set, periodicity, detector structure) were verified by computer for every
circuit. The data set (Data availability) contains the circuit specifications (circuits A, B,
C and Fig.~12), the lists of 4224 weight-11 extended logicals for each basis, the
witnesses (the weight-12 upper-bound witnesses and the weight-11 witnesses for
Fig.~12 and its retimings), the certificates (clause sets of the unsatisfiable
instances), and the reproduction scripts for the lower-bound computations; it is
available at [Zenodo DOI]. Every unsatisfiable clause set in the
data set (129 in total: 104 for the obstruction and optimality instances with all
logicals, and 25 length-2-only instances for Table~\ref{tab:fig12}), and every batch
of the lift computation, is accompanied by a DRAT proof produced by CaDiCaL and
verified with drat-trim; the LRAT core was additionally verified with cake\_lpr.
Here ``accompanied'' means: proofs of up to 100~MB are included in the data set;
larger ones are identified by their SHA-256 hash and can be regenerated
byte-identically with the included script. The certificates cover the solver
decisions only; the circuit template and the reduction of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}
are covered by the pen-and-paper proofs and code-level checks of S5. Limitation:
$\dext=11$ and the completeness of the list of 4224 weight-11 logicals rest on two
independent exhaustive searches; we did not obtain a checkable proof certificate for
them (a direct CNF encoding is beyond CDCL solvers already at weight 6).
