\title{A single-ancilla syndrome circuit with circuit distance 12 for the gross code}

\author{[Name --- PI to fill]}
\affiliation{[Affiliation --- PI to fill]}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The gross code $\code{144,12,12}$ is the flagship bivariate-bicycle code, but what
a syndrome-extraction circuit actually protects against is its circuit distance
$\dcirc$, the minimum number of faults that cause an undetected logical error.
Strikis, Browne and Beverland proved that no non-interleaved single-ancilla circuit
for this code reaches $\dcirc=12$ and asked whether any interleaved circuit can.
We give one: an interleaved single-ancilla circuit with $\dcirc=12$ under standard
circuit-level depolarizing noise, in both memory bases and for every number of
rounds.
The construction keeps the extended code of their distance-11 circuit, and hence its
distance 11, fixed and changes only the order and timing of CNOTs so that none of
the minimum-weight extended-code logicals becomes an undetected circuit error; the
proof combines two short arguments with an exact computation.
The price is a period of 18 time steps per round instead of 8, which is minimal
among colour-symmetric schedules of this kind; along the way we prove that their
original circuit has $\dcirc=11$, as they conjectured.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bivariate-bicycle (BB) codes~\cite{Bravyi2024} are leading candidates for
low-overhead quantum memory; IBM's architecture is built on the gross code, with
parameters $\code{144,12,12}$~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}. A code is only
as good as the circuit that measures its checks: faults inside the circuit spread
to several qubits, and the relevant figure of merit is the circuit distance
$\dcirc$, the minimum number of circuit faults that flip a logical observable
without triggering any detector. At low physical error rates the logical error
rate is governed by $\dcirc$, not by the code distance $d$.

No circuit for the gross code had been shown to reach $\dcirc=d=12$. The IBM
circuit, with 8 time steps per round, has $\dcirc\le10$~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}.
Strikis, Browne and Beverland (SBB)~\cite{Strikis2026} analysed single-ancilla
circuits, in which each check is measured by one dedicated ancilla coupled by CNOTs.
They append to the check matrix the ``residual'' errors that an ancilla fault
spreads onto the data, obtaining an extended code of distance $\dext$, and show
that $\dcirc\ge\dext$ for every such circuit (their Prop.~3), while
$\dcirc\le\dext$ holds when the circuit is \emph{non-interleaved}, i.e.\ when on
every data qubit all $X$-check CNOTs of a round precede all $Z$-check CNOTs or vice
versa (their Cor.~1). Since every CNOT order gives $\dext\le11$ for the gross
code, non-interleaved circuits are capped at 11 (their Prop.~4); their depth-8
circuit (their Fig.~12, hereafter ``Fig.~12'') was conjectured to reach it. They
left open ``whether any interleaved circuit can achieve circuit distance
12''~\cite{Strikis2026}.

We answer this question in the affirmative. The circuit of Table~\ref{tab:circuit}
is single-ancilla, CNOT-based and interleaved, and has $\dcirc=12$ for every number
of rounds (Theorem~\ref{thm:main})\cl{41,40}. The idea (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}) is
to exploit the gap between Prop.~3 and Cor.~1: we keep the extended code of Fig.~12,
with $\dext=11$, and choose CNOT orders and times such that none of its
minimum-weight logicals can be realised by eleven undetected circuit faults. The
cost is time: the period is 18 steps per round instead of 8\cl{44}. The same tools
prove SBB's conjecture that Fig.~12 has $\dcirc=11$\cl{10}.

Exact circuit distances are known for hypergraph-product codes~\cite{ManesClaes},
for the smallest BB circuit from exact solvers~\cite{Webster2026}, and as
certificates for other qLDPC constructions~\cite{Liang2026,SunCai2026}; for larger
BB circuits reported values are typically upper bounds from randomized search. Morphing
circuits with the same qubit count~\cite{ShawTerhal2025,Wills2026} act on a
different $\code{144,12,12}$ code and have no dedicated ancillas. Lower bounds for
interleaved circuits were proposed as future work in Ref.~\cite{Nguyen2026}; our
route through a fixed extended code is different. Position-dependent CNOT orders
are an established tool~\cite{TomitaSvore,KishonyFowler,Strikis2026}\cl{40}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Setting.}
We consider memory experiments in the $Z$ or $X$ basis: data initialisation, $R$
rounds of syndrome extraction and transversal readout. Each check has one ancilla
that is reset, coupled by six CNOTs and measured once per round; rounds of
different checks may overlap in time (pipelining). Detectors compare consecutive
measurements of a check, plus the first round and the final readout. Faults are
the single-operation Pauli components of standard circuit-level depolarizing noise
(SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}: two-qubit depolarization after CNOTs, flips after
resets and before measurements, single-qubit depolarization on idle qubits),
counted as distinct detector-error-model (DEM) mechanisms~\cite{Gidney2021stim}.
$\dcirc$ is the minimum number of faults that flip a logical observable while
triggering no detector. Lower bounds hold for any sub-model (e.g.\ without idle
noise).

For a check with CNOT order $(q_1,\dots,q_6)$, an ancilla fault after the $k$-th
CNOT deposits the residual $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$ on the data. The extended code
of a basis appends all residual columns $(Hs,Ls)$ of the relevant checks to the
data columns ($H$: check matrix, $L$: logical operators). Every single fault,
propagated to the readout, has a data image that is zero or one extended-code
column (SBB, Table~I). For Fig.~12, $\dext=11$ in both bases, with exactly 4224
weight-11 extended logicals per basis, all realisable in Fig.~12 by eleven
undetected faults\cl{11,13}.

\paragraph{Key idea.}
Interleaving decouples $\dcirc$ from $\dext$ in one direction only: $\dcirc\ge\dext$
still holds, but $\dcirc$ can exceed it. To use this, the extended code must stay at
distance 11, and the timing must prevent every weight-11 extended logical from being
realised by undetected faults. The first requirement is met by an invariance. Up to
the check itself, a residual of length 5 equals $\{q_1\}$, of length 4 equals
$\{q_1,q_2\}$, of length 3 equals the complement of $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, and of length
2 is $\{q_5,q_6\}$. The extended code therefore depends only on the three unordered
sets $\{q_1,q_2\}$, $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, $\{q_5,q_6\}$ of each check, which are
preserved by reversal (rev) and by the swaps $s_{12}$, $s_{56}$ of positions 1,2 and
5,6. All eight orders generated by these operations share the extended code of
Fig.~12, its distance 11 and its list of 4224 weight-11 logicals
(Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}a)\cl{32,42}.

The second requirement is a statement about time. Reversing an order keeps the
extended code but changes which physical fault realises each residual column and
when its spread reaches the neighbouring checks. Consider a weight-11 extended
logical made of ten data errors and one two-qubit hook column $\{q_5,q_6\}$ of an
$X$ check (Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}b). In Fig.~12 the column is produced by an ancilla
fault after the fourth CNOT, which lands on the data after the neighbouring $Z$
checks have read those qubits; every data error can then be placed after its reads,
and no detector fires. With the order reversed, the same column can only come from a
fault between the second and third CNOT, whose spread over four qubits (equal to the hook up to
a stabiliser) reaches one neighbouring check after its readout and another before
it. The checks along a cycle through the hook and three data qubits each see two
errors of the logical and stay silent only if both fall on the same side of their
readout; the early and late constraints propagate around the cycle and leave one
check with odd parity, so a detector fires: timing turns the potential weight-11
logical into a parity cycle that cannot be satisfied. For all 360 logicals of this
``ten data plus one hook'' form, the lift fails at $R=1$, and every irreducible
conflicting set of detectors (one whose constraints alone forbid the lift, but no
proper subset does) has between 4 and 10 detectors; the smallest such set has 4, 5
or 9 detectors\cl{49}. Circuit design becomes a finite problem: find orders in the
class and CNOT times for which none of the 4224 known logicals lifts. With the
Fig.~12 orders themselves this is impossible in every family of retimings we could
decide\cl{16}.

\paragraph{Construction.}
We searched schedules that are symmetric under the 24 translations preserving the
colouring, choosing for each of the six (type, colour) classes one of the eight
orders and its CNOT times with a SAT solver; constraints enforce legality (no data
qubit in two CNOTs at once), determinism of all detectors, and blocking of every
weight-11 extended logical in a bulk Pauli-frame model (SM~\ref{sm:search}).
Candidates were then certified exactly. Table~\ref{tab:circuit} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:timing} give the resulting circuit. It measures the gross code
itself, is legal and deterministic, is pipelined (every check's window crosses a
period boundary) and interleaved (96 data qubits switch between $X$- and $Z$-type
CNOTs four times per period, 48 twice); every check's order differs from that of
Fig.~12\cl{41,42}. A second period-18 circuit and a period-21 circuit in which all
ancillas are reset and measured together have the same distance
(SM~\ref{sm:circuits})\cl{31,43}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Under SD6 noise, the circuit of Table~\ref{tab:circuit} has $\dcirc=12$ in both
memory bases for every number of rounds $R\ge1$.
\end{theorem}

The proof has three steps, two of them pen-and-paper and one computer-assisted.

\begin{lemma}[Reduction]\label{lem:reduction}
In any circuit of our template, an undetected fault set of size $w$ that flips a
logical observable yields an extended-code logical of weight at most $w$, formed
by its nonzero fault images.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Add one observable per row of $H$ and $L$, evaluated on the final
data readout. The observable of row $H_i$ equals the sum of all detectors of check
$i$, since these telescope from the first measurement to the final readout. For an
undetected fault set the $H$ parts of the images therefore sum to zero, while the
$L$ parts sum to the logical effect. Each image is zero or one column (SBB,
Table~I), and pairs of equal columns cancel. $\square$
This uses neither Prop.~3 of SBB nor non-interleaving; with $\dext=11$ it excludes
weight $\le10$ for every $R$\cl{33}.

\begin{lemma}[Finite rounds]\label{lem:lift}
For $R=1,\dots,32$ and both bases, none of the 4224 weight-11 extended logicals is
the image of eleven undetected faults.
\end{lemma}
We verified this by an exact computation: for each logical, a CP-SAT model chooses
one DEM mechanism per column subject to every detector sum vanishing; all
$2\times32\times4224$ instances are infeasible\cl{42}. As a positive control, the
same program finds a realisation for all 4224 logicals in Fig.~12. The computation
was carried out with two independent implementations, and the inputs ($\dext=11$
and the list of 4224 logicals) with two independent exhaustive searches
(SM~\ref{sm:dext}, \ref{sm:lift})\cl{11,13}.

\begin{lemma}[All rounds]\label{lem:gap}
If a weight-11 undetected logical existed for some $R$, one would exist for some
$R'\le32$.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof sketch.} After the last fault of a fault set at layer $a$, the data
frame is constant from layer $a+D-1$ on, $D=18$, because only ancillas still in
flight can change it. If an undetected logical had no fault in a window of
$G=2D+\Delta-1=52$ layers, where $\Delta=17$ is the longest reset-to-measurement
span of a check, no detector could respond to both the faults before and after the
window; both parts would be undetected, their observables would add to a nonzero
value, and one part would have weight $\le10$, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}. So a weight-11 logical spans at most $10\times51$ layers.
The noisy circuit is periodic: inserting an 18-layer block at any layer from the
fourth on yields the circuit with one more round. Deleting fault-free blocks before
and after the logical leaves every detector it touches unchanged, which maps it into
a circuit with at most 32 rounds (SM~\ref{sm:gap}). $\square$\cl{43}

Lemmas~\ref{lem:reduction}--\ref{lem:gap} give $\dcirc\ge12$ for every $R$. For the
upper bound, a weight-12 logical operator of the code, applied as single-qubit
flips after data initialisation, triggers no detector and flips the logical
observable (SM~\ref{sm:upper})\cl{42}. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:fig12}
The Fig.~12 circuit of SBB has $\dcirc=11$ in both bases for every $R$.
\end{corollary}
Here $\dcirc\ge\dext=11$ by SBB's Prop.~3 and $\dcirc\le\dext$ by their Cor.~1;
$\dext=11$ is the result of a complete symmetric search over the extended code,
reproduced with an independently built instance and a second symmetry
group\cl{10,11}. Retiming Fig.~12 without changing its orders does not help: among
schedules of period $\le8$ there are four conflict-free ones up to translation, all
with weight-11 undetected errors, and in the bulk, every legal circuit of several
longer-period families keeping the orders has such an error
(SM~\ref{sm:fig12})\cl{14,15,16,17}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Cost and optimality.}
The circuit uses the qubits of the IBM and SBB circuits, one ancilla per check, but
18 time steps per round instead of 8, i.e.\ 2.25 times as many, with more idle
locations\cl{44}. Within the schedules we searched, this period cannot be reduced:
among circuits symmetric under the 24 colour-preserving translations with orders in
the class of Fig.~12, every legal circuit with period $P\le17$ has, for
sufficiently many rounds, a weight-11 undetected error, and the same holds for
$P\le20$ when all ancillas share one reset and measurement frame; the minimal
periods are 18 and 21\cl{45}. Nor is the class of orders arbitrary: among all 24
colourings by cosets of translation subgroups of index 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 or 9, with one
order type per colour, only the class of Fig.~12 has $\dext\ge11$ in both
bases\cl{46}. With less symmetry the minimal period is open: invariance under a
translation subgroup of order 12 already excludes $P\le11$, and any non-trivial
subgroup excludes $P=8$, but schedules without symmetry remain
undecided\cl{47,38}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Discussion.}
The obstruction found by SBB is specific to non-interleaved circuits, not to the
single-ancilla model: the gross code admits a single-ancilla circuit that preserves
its full distance. The method transfers. Fixing the extended code and searching
only over orders within its class and over timing turns an open-ended circuit
search into a lift problem against a finite list of minimum-weight extended
logicals, which can be decided exactly and certified; the same approach applies to
other single-ancilla circuits whose distance is limited by Cor.~1 of SBB.

$\dcirc$ is a property of the circuit, independent of the decoder; whether the
longer period, with its additional idle locations, lowers the logical error rate at
practical noise levels and with practical decoders is not evaluated here. Our
results also do not cover correlated faults, leakage or CZ-native compilations.
The main open question is whether $\dcirc=12$ can be reached at period 8, or at any
period below 18, by less symmetric schedules.

\section{Notation and noise model}\label{sm:notation}
\emph{Code.} The gross code~\cite{Bravyi2024} has $\ell=12$, $m=6$, $A=x^3+y+y^2$,
$B=y^3+x+x^2$, $H_X=[A\,|\,B]$, $H_Z=[B^{T}\,|\,A^{T}]$; qubits and checks are
indexed by $i=6a+b$ for the monomial $x^ay^b$, with left and right blocks of 72
qubits each; $n=144$, $k=12$, $d=12$. Term labels: $A_1=x^3$, $A_2=y$, $A_3=y^2$,
$B_1=y^3$, $B_2=x$, $B_3=x^2$. The $X$ check at $(x,y)$ acts on left qubit
$(x,y)+A_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)+B_k$; the $Z$ check at $(x,y)$ on left qubit
$(x,y)-B_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)-A_k$. The colour of a check is $(x+y)\bmod3$;
the 24 translations $(a,b)$ with $a+b\equiv0\pmod3$ preserve colours.

\emph{Circuit template.} Each check has one ancilla. In every round it is reset,
applies its six CNOTs in its order (ancilla as control for $X$ checks, as target for
$Z$ checks) and is measured. A schedule assigns each check a reset step, six CNOT
steps and a measurement step modulo the period $P$; windows may wrap around the
period boundary, so that rounds of different checks overlap. A $Z$-basis memory
initialises the data in $|0\rangle$, runs $R$ rounds and reads all data in the $Z$
basis; detectors are the first measurement of each $Z$ check, the comparison of
consecutive measurements of each $Z$ check, and the comparison of the last
measurement with the parity of the final readout. The $X$ basis is analogous.

\emph{Noise and $\dcirc$.} SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}: after every CNOT a
two-qubit depolarizing channel; after every reset a flip; before every
measurement a flip; on every qubit idle in a time step a single-qubit depolarizing
channel (also during reset and measurement layers). A fault is one Pauli component
of one channel. Faults with identical detector and observable signatures are one
DEM mechanism, and $\dcirc$ counts mechanisms. For lower bounds we use the CSS
relaxation: in a $Z$-basis memory only $Z$-check detectors and the $X$ part of
each fault are kept; this can only lower the minimum. Upper bounds are checked on
the full circuit. Lower bounds hold for any sub-model of SD6.

\section{Invariance of the extended code}\label{sm:invariance}\cl{32,42}
Consider a $Z$-basis memory; only $X$ errors matter. An $X$ error on the ancilla of
an $X$ check after its $k$-th CNOT propagates to the suffix $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$;
$Z$-check CNOTs (data controls, ancilla target) do not change the $X$ frame on the
data. Each fault's final data $X$ frame is therefore zero, a single data qubit, or a
proper suffix of an $X$-check order, and its column is $(H_Zs,L_Zs)$. Multiplying
by the check itself, which has zero column, a suffix of length 5 is equivalent to
$\{q_1\}$ (a data column), of length 4 to $\{q_1,q_2\}$, of length 3 to the
complement of $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, and a suffix of length 2 is $\{q_5,q_6\}$. Hence the
column set is determined by the three sets $\{q_1,q_2\}$, $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$,
$\{q_5,q_6\}$ of every check. Reversal maps these to $\{q_5,q_6\}$, the last three
and $\{q_1,q_2\}$, which produce the same columns, and $s_{12}$, $s_{56}$ leave them
unchanged. The $X$ basis is symmetric. For circuits A, B and C we also computed the
extended codes directly: both bases have 360 merged columns, and the column sets
coincide with those of Fig.~12.

\section{The extended code: $\dext=11$ and the 4224 weight-11 logicals}\label{sm:dext}\cl{11,13}
The extended code of Fig.~12 has 360 merged columns per basis (144 of check-weight
3, 144 of weight 6, 72 of weight 9). The 24 colour-preserving translations map each
check's order to the order of its image check and preserve the logical classes
(checked by solving $L\circ T=ML+NH$ with $\mathrm{rank}\,M=k$); the columns form 15
orbits.

\emph{Search.} To decide whether $Hx=0$, $Lx\neq0$ has a solution with $|x|\le W$ we
use a complete depth-first search: a minimum-weight solution may be translated so
that it contains the representative of its lowest orbit; the search branches on the
columns covering an unsatisfied check row, excluding earlier siblings, so that the
branches partition the search space; it prunes when the depth plus
$\lceil|s|/w_{\max}\rceil$ exceeds $W$ ($s$ the current syndrome, $w_{\max}$ the
largest column weight) and closes the last step by table look-up. The program was
validated against unpruned meet-in-the-middle enumeration on smaller instances and
by checking that split runs add up to unsplit runs.

\emph{Results.} At $W=10$ there is no solution in either basis
($\approx2\times10^{10}$ nodes per basis), on two independently constructed
instances, and under a second symmetry group of order 8 (45 orbits,
$\approx5\times10^{10}$ nodes per basis); weight 11 is attained. Hence $\dext=11$.
At $W=11$, two different search programs enumerate the same 4224 extended logicals
per basis, all in free orbits of the 24-element group (176 orbits); 360 of them
consist of ten data columns and one residual of length 2.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}}\label{sm:reduction}\cl{33}
Fix a basis, say $Z$, and augment the circuit with one observable for each row of
$H=H_Z$ and of $L=L_Z$, all measured on the final data readout. Each DEM mechanism
then carries, besides its detectors and logical effect, its image: the $(H,L)$
column of its final data frame. Two facts hold. (i) For every check $i$, the sum of
all detectors of $i$ (first measurement, all consecutive comparisons, final
comparison) telescopes to the parity of row $H_i$ on the final readout, i.e.\ to the
augmented observable $H_i$. Hence for an undetected fault set the $H$ parts of the
images sum to zero. (ii) Every nonzero image is one extended-code column (SBB
Table~I): the data frame of a single fault is zero, one data qubit or one residual,
up to checks. So the nonzero images of an undetected fault set of size $w$ sum to a
vector with zero $H$ part and with $L$ part equal to its logical effect; after
cancelling equal pairs, they form an extended logical of weight $\le w$. If $w=11$
and $\dext=11$, the eleven images are nonzero, distinct and form one of the listed
weight-11 logicals. Mechanisms of the original DEM are unions of augmented
mechanisms, so the bound transfers. Neither (i) nor (ii) uses non-interleaving or
round separation. The $Z$-basis template has no $X$-check detectors; fewer detectors
only enlarge the set of undetected fault sets, so the lower bound also holds with
all detectors. We checked (i) and (ii) on the DEM of every circuit and every $R$ used
in S6, in both bases: each mechanism's per-check detector parity equals the $H$ part
of its image, its logical effect equals the $L$ part, and the image set equals the
360 extended columns.

\section{Computation for Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}}\label{sm:lift}\cl{34,42}
By S5, a weight-11 undetected logical selects, for each of the eleven columns of
one listed extended logical, one DEM mechanism with that image, such that the
detector sums vanish. For each of the 4224 logicals per basis, a CP-SAT model has a
Boolean variable per mechanism of the eleven image groups, an exactly-one
constraint per group, and an XOR constraint per detector. Infeasibility for all 4224
logicals at a given $R$ means that no weight-11 undetected logical exists at that
$R$.

Results: circuit A, $R=1,\dots,32$, both bases: all infeasible. A second,
independently written lift program agrees and extends the range to $R=36$. Circuit
B: $R=1,\dots,24$; circuit C: $R=1,\dots,14$ and $16$, plus a solver-free
meet-in-the-middle check (split the eleven groups in two halves, enumerate all
detector sums of each half, intersect) for $R\le3$; all infeasible. Positive
control: on Fig.~12 the same programs find a realisation for all 4224 logicals.

\section{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gap}}\label{sm:gap}\cl{35,43}
Notation: period $D=18$; check $q$ has start $s_q$, instance $j$ is reset at
$s_q+jD$ and measured at $s_q+jD+\Delta_q$ with $\Delta_q\le D-1$; $\Delta=\max_q
\Delta_q=17$; $S=\max_q s_q-\min_q s_q=5$. Faults are attached to layers.

\emph{(1) Settling.} If all faults of a set $F_<$ lie at layers $\le a$, the data
frame they produce is constant from layer $a+D-1$ on: only an ancilla fault can
later change the data, through the remaining CNOTs of its own instance, all at
layers $\le a+D-2$; a freshly reset ancilla never pushes errors onto the data (an
$X$-check ancilla only picks up $Z$ errors from the data, a $Z$-check ancilla only
$X$ errors). Every instance reset after $a+D-1$ is fault-free and measures $H_ce$.
If all faults of $F_>$ lie at layers $\ge b$, measurements before $b$ are unaffected.

\emph{(2) Splitting.} Let $F=F_<\cup F_>$ be a weight-11 undetected logical with no
fault strictly between $a$ and $b$. A comparison detector $(c,j)$ responds to $F_<$
only if $s(c,j-1)<a+D-1$ and to $F_>$ only if the measurement of instance $j$, at
$s(c,j-1)+D+\Delta_c\le a+2D+\Delta-2$, is at a layer $\ge b$. The first-round
detectors are analogous, and a final detector responds to both only if
$b-a<D+S+\Delta$. Hence for $b-a\ge G=\max(2D+\Delta-1,\,D+S+\Delta)=52$ no detector
responds to both parts; each part is undetected, their logical effects add to a
nonzero value, and one part has weight $\le10$, contradicting Lemma~1. Consecutive
faults of $F$ are therefore less than $G$ layers apart, and $F$ spans at most
$10(G-1)=510$ layers.

\emph{(3) Deletion.} The noisy SD6 circuit with $R$ rounds equals the circuit with
$R-1$ rounds with an 18-layer block inserted at layer $x$, for every
$x\in[4,18(R-1)]$ (checked layer by layer in both bases; the template is exactly
periodic). Removing a block $[x,x+D)$ that contains no fault of $F$ removes one
measurement of every ancilla; a detector of the shorter circuit that does not
straddle the cut equals a detector of the longer one, and one that straddles it
equals the sum of the two detectors around the removed measurement. $F$ therefore
stays undetected, with the same logical effect, provided the frame entering layer
$x+D$ of the longer circuit equals the frame entering layer $x$ of the shorter one;
this holds if $x+D\le f_1$ (all zero; $f_1$ the first fault layer) or
$x\ge f_{11}+2D-2$ (data frame settled by (1), all ancillas in flight fault-free).
Deleting all such blocks maps $F$ into a circuit with at most 32 rounds. With
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}, no weight-11 undetected logical exists for any $R$.

\emph{Circuits B and C.} For circuit C, rounds are separated in time; a fault-free
round inside the span of $F$ splits it as in (2), so $F$ occupies at most 11
consecutive rounds, and periodicity reduces any $R$ to $R\le13$. For circuit B we use
a slab version of (2): a fault-free slab from the earliest reset to the latest
measurement of one round splits $F$, provided that on every data qubit every
propagating CNOT of round $k$ inside the overlap with round $k+1$ precedes every
reading CNOT of round $k+1$. We verified this condition for all rounds of the
circuits with $R=3,8,24$ in both bases; it reduces any $R$ to $R\le23$.

\section{Fig.~12 of SBB: $\dcirc=11$ and retimings}\label{sm:fig12}\cl{10,13,14,15,16,17,18}
\emph{$\dcirc=11$.} Fig.~12 is non-interleaved, so SBB's Prop.~3 and Cor.~1 give
$\dcirc=\dext$ for every $R$; $\dext=11$ by S4. The weight-11 extended logicals are
realised by eleven circuit faults (checked by injection for $R=1,2,3$ in both bases).

\emph{Lift structure.} All 4224 weight-11 extended logicals per basis lift to
undetected circuit errors of Fig.~12 ($R=1,2,3$).

\emph{Retimings with period $\le8$.} Consider periodic schedules with period
$P\le8$ shared by all checks, one reset, six CNOTs at distinct offsets and one
measurement per check and period, and no data qubit in two CNOTs at one step.
Weight 6 forces $P=8$ and all offsets; keeping the Fig.~12 orders leaves one start
per check. A backtracking search without symmetry assumptions finds exactly 32
conflict-free assignments, 4 up to global translation (two SAT encodings agree); all
four are colour-uniform, deterministic and non-interleaved, and one is Fig.~12 itself.
For each of them and every $R=1,\dots,6$ in both bases we injected a weight-11 error
that fires no detector and flips the observable.

\emph{Longer periods (bulk).} For longer periods we use a Pauli-frame model of the
bulk: a data error at time $\theta$ is first seen by $Z$ check $c$ in the first
instance whose readout is later than $\theta$; the readouts of three checks on a data
qubit give pairwise difference constraints on these instance indices, and a hook
residual fixes its own arrival time. Feasibility of the constraints implies a genuine
weight-11 undetected error when the number of rounds is large enough (confirmed by
direct injection); the model agrees with the exact lift in the bulk but not at the
initial time boundary. Blocking every ``ten data + one residual'' logical then

SAT loop. The resulting status for retimings that keep the Fig.~12 orders is given

\section{Search and optimality}\label{sm:search}\cl{38,39,45,46,47}
\emph{Search.} The circuits were found with SAT models over schedules symmetric
under the 24 colour-preserving translations. Variables: for each of the six
classes, one of the eight orders of the Fig.~12 class, a start and six CNOT times;
constraints: legality (six distinct times modulo $P$ per data qubit), determinism
(on every overlapping pair of $X$ and $Z$ checks, the number of shared qubits on
which the $X$ instance acts before the $Z$ instance is even, for every instance
offset), and, added lazily, one clause per frame-model-liftable weight-11 extended
logical forbidding the timing that makes it liftable. Models were then certified
with S5--S7. With the Fig.~12 orders pinned the model is unsatisfiable, in line
with S9. As a control, 233 random legal deterministic schedules of the
common-frame family, sampled without the blocking clauses, all have liftable
weight-11 logicals.

\emph{Minimum period.} With the same model and no circuit fixed, pipelined schedules
with $P=8,\dots,17$ and common-frame schedules with $P=20$ are unsatisfiable. For the
pipelined case a second, independent encoding (absolute times, one-sided difference
literals) agrees for $P=8,\dots,17$. The final clause sets were re-solved from scratch
by independent SAT solvers (three for $P=20$ common frame and $P=12$, two for
$P=17$). Completeness of the family encoding: at $P=8$ it enumerates exactly the 944
legal deterministic circuits found by an independent backtracking search, and 75
independently sampled legal deterministic circuits at $P=12$, 17 (pipelined) and 20
(common frame) are all models. Soundness of the clauses: in 991 sampled cases,
including logicals with several residual columns, every lift asserted by the frame
model was confirmed on the exact DEM, and in 99 sampled cases every violated clause
corresponded to an exact lift. Unsatisfiability means that every legal deterministic
circuit of the family has a weight-11 undetected error for sufficiently many rounds;
it is not a statement at fixed small $R$. Restricting the CNOT window of class $X_0$
or of class $Z_2$ to at most 14 steps is unsatisfiable at $P=24$, while each of the
other four classes can be so restricted; since a window spans at most $P-3$ steps,
this suggests why $P\ge18$ is needed, but the bound itself rests on the direct runs.
At $P=8$ all 944 legal deterministic circuits of the family were also checked
directly: each has a weight-11 undetected error at $R=1$ in the $Z$ basis.

\emph{Uniqueness of the order class.} Per check, the extended-code columns are the
data columns and three residual sets; up to reversal there are 90 types per basis, of
which 45 contain a residual of length 3 that, together with nine data columns, forms
a weight-10 extended logical. For each of the 24 colourings by cosets of translation
subgroups of index 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 9 (3, 4, 3, 12, 1 and 1 subgroups), with one type
per colour, a SAT search with witness clauses leaves only the translates of Fig.~12
with $\dext\ge11$ in both bases; every witness used was re-verified as at most ten
distinct columns of that assignment's extended code with vanishing $H$ part and
nonzero $L$ part. Colourings of index 12, 18, 24 and 36 and per-check-free
assignments are not covered.

\emph{Less symmetry.} Pipelined schedules invariant under the order-12 subgroup
generated by $(1,2)$ are obstructed for every $P\le11$, and schedules invariant
under any subgroup of order $\ge2$ are obstructed at $P=8$. Schedules without
symmetry, and the remaining subgroups at $P=12,\dots,17$, are open.
