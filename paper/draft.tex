\title{Minimum period of distance-preserving syndrome extraction for bivariate-bicycle codes}

\author{Guancheng Pan}
\email{3250101086@zju.edu.cn}
\affiliation{Zhejiang University, Hangzhou, China}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Single-ancilla syndrome-extraction circuits for the gross code $\code{144,12,12}$ and the
$\code{90,8,10}$ bivariate-bicycle code lose circuit distance unless overlapping $X$ and
$Z$ checks are interleaved; how short a distance-preserving circuit can be was open. For
translation-invariant circuits, in which all checks of one type share a CNOT timing
repeated with period $P$, under circuit-level depolarizing noise, the shortest period
preserving the code distance is 10 for the gross code and 11 for $\code{90,8,10}$, and we
find the best circuit distance at every shorter period. The circuits work for every number
of rounds, the impossibility results for sufficiently many. Interleaving acts through an
integer cochain of round offsets: a circuit can be re-timed into a non-interleaved one
exactly when this cochain is a coboundary, and a light extended-code logical is blocked
when difference constraints along its hooks form a negative cycle. The price is idling:
under standard idle noise the full-distance circuits are worse than IBM's depth-8
circuits at all directly resolvable error rates.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bivariate-bicycle (BB) codes~\cite{Bravyi2024} are leading candidates for
low-overhead quantum memory; IBM's architecture is built on the gross code
$\code{144,12,12}$~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}. What a syndrome circuit delivers is
its circuit distance $\dcirc$, the minimum number of circuit faults that flip a logical
observable without triggering a detector; at low physical error rates it governs the
logical error rate. Single-ancilla circuits, in which each check is measured by one
ancilla coupled to its data qubits by CNOTs, are the hardware-minimal choice, and the
number of time steps per round, the period, sets how long every qubit idles. The design
question is therefore the shortest period at which $\dcirc$ equals the code distance $d$.

Strikis, Browne and Beverland (SBB)~\cite{Strikis2026} append to the check matrix the
residual errors that ancilla faults spread onto the data and obtain an extended code of
distance $\dext$. Every single-ancilla circuit has $\dcirc\ge\dext$ (their Prop.~3), and
a \emph{non-interleaved} circuit, in which all $X$-type CNOTs precede all overlapping
$Z$-type CNOTs or vice versa, has $\dcirc\le\dext$ (their Cor.~1). For the gross code
every choice of CNOT orders gives $\dext\le11$, so non-interleaved circuits are capped at
11 (their Prop.~4, by a numerical search over orders), and uniformly tiled ones at 10
(Prop.~5); for $\code{90,8,10}$ every choice of orders gives $\dext\le8$
(SM~\ref{sm:earlier})\cl{L-22}. IBM's depth-8 circuits have circuit distance at most 10 and
8~\cite{Bravyi2024}, values confirmed for fixed numbers of
rounds~\cite{Tesseract2025,Crocker2026}; we show that they are exact for every number of
rounds (Table~\ref{tab:periods}). Interleaved circuits can do better, but whether
they can reach the full distance, and at what period, was open~\cite{Strikis2026}.
Known lower bounds on syndrome-circuit depth concern only the correctness of a single
round: depth 6 is impossible and 7 is optimal for these
codes~\cite{Li2025parity,Zhang2026ASC,DurazoRocha2026}.
Nothing was known about how short an interleaved circuit can be when it must also
preserve distance, nor why interleaving helps at all.

We answer both questions within the natural class $\T(P)$ of translation-invariant
single-ancilla circuits of period $P$ (Definition~1). The minimum period with $\dcirc=d$
is exactly 10 for the gross code and 11 for $\code{90,8,10}$; at period 8 the best
circuit distances are 11 and 8 (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Table~\ref{tab:periods})\cl{L-2,L-3,L-5}.
The circuits reach these values for every number of rounds $R$ and both memory bases; the
impossibility results hold for sufficiently many rounds. The mechanism is an integer
cochain $\delta$ that records, for every overlapping pair of checks, in which round one
reads the errors the other deposits. A circuit can be re-timed into a non-interleaved one
exactly when $\delta$ is a coboundary (Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}); whether a light
extended logical survives as an undetected fault set is a system of difference
constraints, which a negative cycle along two hooks can make infeasible
(Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, Corollary~\ref{cor:cycle}); and at period 8 a flat
two-qubit hook costs one unit of distance because every pair of check qubits lies in a
minimum-weight logical (Lemma~\ref{lem:pair}).

Exact circuit distances and certified bounds exist for hypergraph-product
codes~\cite{ManesClaes}, the smallest BB circuit~\cite{Webster2026} and other qLDPC
constructions~\cite{Liang2026,Ghosh2026}; morphing circuits~\cite{ShawTerhal2025,Wills2026}
and pipelined circuits~\cite{Xu2023} are other routes to short rounds, and interleaving
$X$ and $Z$ checks recovers the full distance of the surface code at minimum
depth~\cite{Hirai2026}. Hooks aligned with minimum-weight logicals cost distance since the
surface code~\cite{Dennis2002}, and check-dependent CNOT orders are the usual
remedy~\cite{TomitaSvore,KishonyFowler,Strikis2026}. For overlapping $X$ and $Z$ checks a
single round is correct only if an even number of shared qubits see the $X$ CNOT
first~\cite{Li2025parity,Zhang2026ASC,DurazoRocha2026}; $\delta$ is the integer, multi-round refinement of
this $\mathbb Z_2$ parity\cl{L-21}.

\paragraph{Setting.}
We consider memory experiments in the $Z$ or $X$ basis: data initialisation, $R$ rounds
of syndrome extraction and transversal readout. Faults are the single-operation Pauli
components of circuit-level depolarizing noise with idle errors
(SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}); faults with identical detector and observable effects
are merged into one detector-error-model mechanism~\cite{Gidney2021stim}, and $\dcirc$
counts mechanisms, minimised over both bases. In a $Z$-basis memory only $X$ errors
matter: an error on an $X$-check ancilla after its $k$-th CNOT deposits the
\emph{residual} $\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$ of its order on the data (a \emph{hook}), and
the extended code appends the columns $(Hs,Ls)$ of all residuals $s$ to the data columns
($H$: check matrix, $L$: logicals); its distance is $\dext$~\cite{Strikis2026}. A
BB code has checks $c\mapsto c+A_i$, $c+B_j$ ($i,j=1,2,3$), and every $X$ check
overlaps exactly nine $Z$ checks $c+A_i+B_j$, on two qubits each (SM~\ref{sm:class}).

\begin{definition}[class $\T(P)$]
A schedule is in $\T(P)$ if (i) every check has one ancilla, which is reset, coupled to
its six data qubits by CNOTs and measured, in that order; (ii) all $X$ checks share CNOT
steps $t_X(\ell)$, $\ell\in\{A_1,\dots,B_3\}$, all $Z$ checks share $t_Z(\ell)$, and round
$j$ adds $jP$; (iii) the six steps of each type are distinct and span at most $P-3$, leaving
one step for reset and one for measurement; (iv) the six CNOTs on every data qubit fall on
distinct steps modulo $P$; (v) the circuit measures the checks, i.e.\ on every overlapping
pair of $X$ and $Z$ checks both shared qubits see the same relative round. CNOT orders are
arbitrary and rounds may wrap around the period.
\end{definition}
\noindent Condition (v) is necessary for a deterministic circuit, $\T(P)$ is empty for
$P<8$, and IBM's depth-8 circuits lie in $\T(8)$\cl{L-1}.

\begin{theorem}[optimal periods]\label{thm:main}
Under SD6 noise and for every sufficiently large number of rounds, every circuit in
$\T(8)$ or $\T(9)$ has $\dcirc\le11$ for the gross code, and every circuit in $\T(8)$
has $\dcirc\le8$ and in $\T(9)$ or $\T(10)$ has $\dcirc\le9$ for $\code{90,8,10}$. Each of
these values, and $\dcirc=d$ at periods 10 (gross) and 11 ($\code{90,8,10}$), is attained
in both memory bases for every $R\ge1$ by the circuits of Table~\ref{tab:periods}.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Hence, in $\T$, the minimum period with $\dcirc=d$ is 10 for the gross code and
11 for $\code{90,8,10}$\cl{L-2,L-3,L-5,L-23}.

\paragraph{The round-offset cochain.}
Fix a $Z$-basis memory. A data error at time $u$ on qubit $q$ is seen by $Z$ check $z\ni q$
first in instance $\sigma_z(q,u)=\min\{t: \rho_z(q)+tP>u\}$, where $\rho_z(q)$ is the step
at which instance 0 of $z$ acts on $q$; it flips exactly the detector comparing instances
$\sigma-1$ and $\sigma$. A set of faults is undetected iff at every check the indices it
produces pair up, and every SD6 fault is a data error, a hook or a readout flip of this
staircase model (SM~\ref{sm:model})\cl{L-24}. An $X$-check ancilla error before the first
CNOT deposits the whole check and is invisible, so for an $X$ check $c$ and a $Z$ check
$z$ the two shared qubits receive the same index from instance 0 of $c$; we call it
$\delta(c,z)$.

\begin{proposition}[round-offset cochain]\label{prop:delta}
Let every overlapping $X$ and $Z$ check share two qubits. (a) The instances can be
relabelled so that every $X$ instance acts on each shared qubit between two consecutive
instances of each overlapping $Z$ check iff $\delta(c,z)=b_z-a_c$ for integers $a,b$,
i.e.\ $\delta$ is a coboundary. (b) Then every extended logical is realised by undetected
faults for sufficiently many rounds, so $\dcirc\le\dext$. (c) If $\delta$ is constant in the round
labelling of the memory circuit, (b) holds for every $R$; this is SBB's Cor.~1. For a circuit in $\T$, $\delta$ is a $3\times3$
matrix $\delta(A_i,B_j)$, a coboundary iff its nine entries are equal.
\end{proposition}
\noindent Relabelling changes $\delta$ by a coboundary, which gives (a); for (b) each
hook is taken in instance $a_c$ and each data error just before its first $X$ CNOT, so
every check receives one common index an even number of times (End Matter).
Interleaving in the cohomological sense is thus necessary for $\dcirc>\dext$ in the bulk, but it is
not sufficient for $\dcirc=d$: IBM's gross circuit has a non-coboundary $\delta$ and
$\dcirc=10$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:idea}a)\cl{L-10,L-11}.

Short periods fail for a structural reason. For a hook residual $S$ let $E(S)$ be the
overlapping $Z$ checks it meets an odd number of times; the hook is \emph{flat} if
$\delta$ is constant on $E(S)$.
\begin{lemma}[pair obstruction]\label{lem:pair}
Let a circuit be in $\T(P)$ and let $S$ be the residual of a hook that leaves the last two
qubits of an $X$-check order, or the last four (equal, modulo the check, to the first two).
If $S$ is flat and the two qubits lie in a weight-$d$ logical, then for sufficiently many
rounds $d-1$ faults form an undetected logical. For the gross code and for $\code{90,8,10}$
every pair of qubits of a check lies in a weight-$d$ logical, for both check types.
\end{lemma}
\noindent The hook replaces two data errors of the logical, and flatness lets all other
data errors be read in the hook's round (Fig.~\ref{fig:idea}c; the $X$ basis is symmetric
with the $Z$-check order). The general form costs $|L|-|T|+1+\min_v\sum_{e\in E(S)}
|\delta(e)-v|$ faults (End Matter). A non-interleaved circuit in $\T$ has constant
$\delta$, so all its hooks are flat and Lemma~\ref{lem:pair} gives $\dcirc\le d-1$
directly; within $\T$ this is the mechanism behind SBB's Prop.~4, there obtained by
numerical search\cl{L-12}.

What decides is whether the light extended logicals can be realised. Fixing how each
column of an extended logical $S$ is realised (as a data error or a hook) and how the
indices pair at each check, the hooks' instances $x$ and the data errors' indices must
satisfy constraints $x_a-x_b\le c_{ab}$: a hook contributes $x_c+\delta(c,z)$ at each check
it meets oddly, a data error any monotone staircase of drop at most one across its three
readers. The window conditions require that each check's instance $-1$ reads before the
first layer and instance $R$ after the last; they hold for every circuit we use but not
in general.

\begin{theorem}[lifting criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Fix a memory basis and a target $t\le d$ with $t\le\dext+2$. Assume the window conditions
and that no hook residual has zero image. If, for every $w$ with $\dext\le w\le t-1$, no
extended logical of weight $w$ together with at most $t-1-w$ readout flips is realised by
undetected faults in the bulk, then $\dcirc\ge t$ for every $R\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[negative cycles]\label{cor:cycle}
For fixed realisations and pairings, realisability is infeasible iff the constraint graph
has a negative cycle. In particular, if $S$ contains hooks of $X$ checks $c,c'$ and two $Z$
checks $z,z'$ meet no other column of $S$, then $S$ is not realised whenever
$\delta(c,z)-\delta(c',z)\neq\delta(c,z')-\delta(c',z')$.
\end{corollary}
\noindent The criterion is a finite, $R$-independent computation that turns
SBB's qualitative observation that interleaving lets part of a propagating error be
detected into a decidable statement\cl{L-13,L-14}. The two-hook certificate is a nonzero
holonomy of $\delta$ around a 4-cycle of the overlap graph; Fig.~\ref{fig:idea}(b) shows it
for $u_{11}$, whose $\delta=-[j\neq B_1]$ blocks all 144 weight-10 extended logicals with
holonomy 2\cl{L-9}. For the other attaining circuits some or all light logicals need the full
difference system with data slack and pairing branches.

\paragraph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.}
A circuit in $\T(P)$ is described in the bulk by its signature, the two CNOT orders and
$\delta$; up to time translation and relabelling there are 22,608, 784,944 and 10,271,304
legal timing vectors at $P=8$, 9 and 10, with 17,424, 67,536 and 267,984 signatures
(SM~\ref{sm:class})\cl{L-1}.

\emph{Period 8.} Every signature of $\T(8)$ has a flat pair hook, the first or last pair
of the $X$ or $Z$ order, so Lemma~\ref{lem:pair} gives $\dcirc\le d-1$ for both codes; an
explicit weight-$(d-1)$ witness was constructed for every legal timing vector
(SM~\ref{sm:p8})\cl{L-12}. For $\code{90,8,10}$ the stronger bound 8 comes from the
patterns below.

\emph{Periods 9 and 10.} Explicit undetected fault patterns, a pair hook with re-timed data
errors or several hooks lifting a light extended logical, cover every legal timing vector
of $\T(8)$ and $\T(9)$ for both codes and of $\T(10)$ for $\code{90,8,10}$, with weights
11 (gross) and 8, 9, 9. Each pattern's residual is a nontrivial logical, and each is
confirmed by injection into the memory circuit. Coverage is certified twice: the
satisfiability encodings are refuted by CaDiCaL~\cite{CaDiCaL2024} with DRAT proofs checked
by drat-trim~\cite{DRATtrim2014}, and an
exhaustive check without SAT runs over all canonical timing vectors. In sampled cases the
witnesses are already undetected for $R\ge2$ (SM~\ref{sm:p910})\cl{L-2,L-15}.

\emph{Attaining circuits.} For $g_{10}$ (Table~\ref{tab:timing}) Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}
applies with $t=12=\dext+2$: the window conditions hold, no hook has zero image, and none of
the 3,204 weight-10 extended logicals (with at most one readout flip) and 121,104 of weight
11 is realised in the bulk, in either basis; a weight-12 data logical applied after
initialisation gives the upper bound. An exhaustive search of the full detector error
model, which uses neither the extended code nor the criterion, finds no undetected logical
of weight $\le11$ for $R=1$, 2, 3 in both bases\cl{L-3}. $c_{11}$ is treated in the same way
($\dext=8$, lists of 585 and 16,830), and a second proof combines exact lifts for
$R\le25$ with a gap argument (End Matter)\cl{L-5}. Every infeasibility claim used in the
proofs for $g_{10}$, $c_{11}$ and the impossibility results is accompanied by a checked
certificate (VeriPB~\cite{Bogaerts2023,GochtNordstrom2021} for $\dext$ and list
completeness, DRAT with LRAT cores checked by cake\_lpr~\cite{Tan2021cakelpr} for the lifts); the encodings are generated by our code and cross-checked against
independent implementations, and the reductions are the mathematical arguments given here,
whose premises were verified computationally\cl{L-15}. The remaining circuits of
Table~\ref{tab:periods} satisfy the criterion with $t\le\dext+2$ by two independent solvers
and a direct search for $R\le2$, 3 or 4; their upper bounds are explicit witnesses at $R=1$ that
persist to every $R$ (End Matter)\cl{L-4,L-6,L-7,L-23}. In particular IBM's circuits have
$\dcirc=10$ and 8 for every $R$, and $g_8$ exceeds them by one at the same period, equal to
SBB's non-interleaved Fig.~12, which lies outside $\T$\cl{L-8}.

\emph{Optimality within a signature.} A signature is realisable at period $P$ iff a
difference system has no positive cycle; the signatures of $u_{11}$ and $c_{11}$ have
minimum period exactly 11 (End Matter)\cl{L-16}. The gain at $P=10$ therefore comes from a
different offset pattern ($\delta$ of $g_{10}$ in Fig.~\ref{fig:idea}a), not from compressing
the period-11 one. $\square$

\paragraph{Cost.}
The extra steps of the period are idle steps: two per ancilla and round for $g_{10}$, three
for $c_{11}$. Under SD6 with idle noise and BP-OSD decoding, the full-distance circuits have
a higher per-round logical error rate than IBM's depth-8 circuits throughout the directly
resolvable range: for the gross code by a factor of about 5--10 at $p=2\times10^{-3}$ and
at least about 1.5 down to $p=1.5\times10^{-3}$ ($g_{10}$ itself is about 2.3 times below
$u_{11}$ at $2\times10^{-3}$), and for $\code{90,8,10}$ at all $p\ge10^{-3}$. Where, below
$10^{-3}$, they would overtake IBM's circuits depends on the smallest number of faults at
which BP-OSD fails on each circuit, which our data do not resolve; plausible extrapolations
place the crossover anywhere from about $10^{-3}$ to below $10^{-4}$, or rule it out. In 23 of 24
sampled failures BP-OSD returned a correction less likely than the actual fault set, so at
these error rates the comparison is between circuit--decoder pairs rather than distances.
Per round, IBM's $\code{90,8,10}$ circuit has exactly 315 ($Z$) and 540 ($X$) undetected
logicals of weight 8, while $c_{11}$ has none below weight 10\cl{L-17}. When idling is free ($p_{\rm idle}=0$) the gain is visible:
$c_{11}$ has a lower per-round logical error rate than IBM's circuit at every sampled $p$
from $1.2\times10^{-3}$ (ratio 0.51 [0.31, 0.83], BP-OSD) to $5\times10^{-3}$ (with both
BP-OSD and Relay-BP from $2\times10^{-3}$; 0.52 [0.44, 0.62] at $3\times10^{-3}$), while at
$p_{\rm idle}=0.1p$ the two are indistinguishable\cl{L-18}. A neutral-atom model with
literature parameters gives $c_{11}$ the lower rate (ratios 0.40--0.82) provided the two
ancilla arrays move one after the other and atoms left unpaired during entangling pulses do
not err; with error on unpaired atoms and simultaneous moves the ordering reverses
(SM~\ref{sm:na})\cl{L-19}.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
Within translation-invariant single-ancilla circuits with one CNOT timing per check type,
separate reset and measurement steps, and SD6 noise, the minimum distance-preserving
period of both codes is now known, and so is the best distance at every shorter period.
The lower bounds come from explicit fault patterns, and period 8 is excluded by one
structural property, the pair covering of Lemma~\ref{lem:pair}. The upper bounds come from
a criterion that is local in time and global over the light extended logicals. The same
period-11 timing template reaches $\dcirc=10$ on $\code{108,8,10}$ but fails on
$\code{90,8,10}$ with the same labels, so term labels must be chosen per code
(SM~\ref{sm:transfer})\cl{L-20}.

Four questions remain open. Can per-check timings, outside $\T$, reach full distance at
period 8 or 9? Can merged measure-and-reset steps, which shorten every period by one, do the
same? Does a criterion like Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} hold for $d\ge\dext+3$, needed for
larger codes, and are the window conditions necessary? And where does the logical error
rate of a full-distance circuit cross that of a shorter one under a decoder whose low-weight
failures are resolved? An analytic proof of the bounds at periods 9 and 10 would also
replace the computer-assisted part of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\emph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:delta}.} Relabelling instance $j$ of $X$ check $c$ as
$j+a_c$ and of $Z$ check $z$ as $j+b_z$ changes $\delta$ to $\delta-(b_z-a_c)$, and
$\delta\equiv0$ is the statement of (a). If $\delta\equiv0$, every $X$ CNOT on $q$ lies in
$(\rho_z(q)-P,\rho_z(q))$ for every reader $z$, so a data error just before the first of them
has index 0 at all readers; taking hooks in instance $a_c$, every check $z$ receives index
$b_z$ exactly $(HS)_z$ times, an even number. If $\delta$ is constant in the round labelling
of the memory circuit, all these faults lie in one round and exist for every $R$, which is
(c). A translation-invariant coboundary is constant after summing over translations.
$\square$

\emph{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:pair}.} Let $T\in\{S,S^c\}$ be the pair inside $L$ and $v$ a
value minimising $\sum_{e\in E(S)}|\delta(e)-v|$. Place every qubit of $L\setminus T$ after
its last read, all at one index $s_0$, and the hook in the instance with index $s_0$ at
value $v$. A check outside $E(S)$ receives 0 or 2 equal hook indices and an even number of
data indices $s_0$; a check $e\in E(S)$ receives one hook index and an odd number of $s_0$,
mismatched by $|\delta(e)-v|$, which as many readout flips repair. The residual is $L$ or
$L$ plus the check, a nontrivial logical, with $|L|-|T|+1+\sum|\delta(e)-v|$ faults; for
a flat pair hook and $|L|=d$ this is $d-1$. $\square$

\emph{Light logicals.} Let an undetected logical consist of $w$ faults, $z$ of zero image
and $c$ pairs of equal nonzero images. Summing all detectors of each check telescopes to
the check's parity on the final readout, so the remaining $w-z-2c$ distinct columns form
an extended logical $S$, and $|F|=|S|+z+2c$ with $|S|\ge\dext$. For $w\le\dext+1$ this forces
$c=0$ and $z\le w-\dext$. Without zero-image hooks the zero-image faults are readout flips.

\emph{Window lemma.} The finite index of a deposit is the number of reads of instances
$0,\dots,R-1$ before it ($R$ for the final readout). If instance $-1$ of every check reads
before the first layer and instance $R$ after the last, the finite index equals the bulk
index for every deposit inside the circuit. Hence every undetected set at finite $R$ is an
undetected bulk set with the same columns (inclusion only: the bulk may use instances
outside $0,\dots,R-1$).

\emph{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.} An undetected logical $F$ with $|F|\le t-1\le
\dext+1$ at some $R$ embeds in the bulk by the window lemma; by the count above it realises
an extended logical of weight $w\in[\dext,t-1]$ with at most $t-1-w$ readout flips, which
the hypothesis excludes. $\square$

\emph{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:cycle}.} A hook of instance $x_c$ contributes
$x_c+\delta(c,z)$ at each check $z\in E(S)$; a data error on $q$ contributes indices that,
ordered cyclically by read time, satisfy $y_1\ge y_2\ge y_3\ge y_1-1$; equal indices at a
check are paired, and a readout flip adds $\{s,s+1\}$. All constraints are of the form
$x_a-x_b\le c_{ab}$, feasible iff no negative cycle exists. At a check met only by hooks
$c,c'$, pairing forces $x_c-x_{c'}=\delta(c',z)-\delta(c,z)$; two such checks with different
right sides give a cycle of weight $-|h|$, $h$ the holonomy. With $|h|\ge2$ one readout flip
cannot close it. $\square$

\emph{Certificates for $g_{10}$.} $\dext=10$ and the completeness of the lists of 3,204
weight-10 and 121,104 weight-11 extended logicals are certified in both bases by VeriPB
proofs. Bulk realisability reduces to a finite instance by a compression lemma: in the
round labelling of the memory circuit every fault of $g_{10}$ spans at most one index; in
an undetected realisation of at most 11 faults, equal indices at a check link faults into
components, each component can be shifted by whole rounds independently, and here only one
component exists (otherwise a part would be a lighter logical or trivial). Its indices
span at most 11 and shift into $[1,12]$, which avoids the first-round and final detectors
of the circuit with $R^*=13$ rounds; the window lemma then makes the finite and bulk
indices equal. At $R=13$ none of the 248,616 list items (both bases, no symmetry
reduction) is realised, certified by DRAT proofs checked with drat-trim and, for their LRAT
cores, cake\_lpr\cl{L-25}.

\emph{Gap argument for $c_{11}$.} Every check instance of $c_{11}$ starts at an offset
$0$ or $1$ modulo $D=11$, is measured 10 layers later and has its last CNOT at offset 9.
If all faults of a set lie at layers $\le a$, the data frame they leave is constant from
layer $a+D-1$ on; a detector responds to faults before $a$ and after $b$ only if $b-a\le30$.
A fault-free window of 31 layers therefore splits an undetected logical into two undetected
parts, one of them lighter. With no undetected logical of weight $\le w-1$, one of weight
$w$ spans at most $30(w-1)$ layers. The circuit with $R$ rounds equals the circuit with
$R-1$ rounds with an 11-layer block inserted at any layer $x\in[1,11(R-1)]$, so deleting
fault-free blocks maps every undetected logical of weight 8 (9) into a circuit with at
most 22 (25) rounds. Exact lifts of the 585 and 16,830 extended logicals for $R\le25$, both
bases, are zero and certified by DRAT proofs; $\dext\ge8$ and the list completeness are
certified by VeriPB\cl{L-5,L-15}.

\emph{Persistence of upper bounds.} The memory circuit with $R\ge R_0$ rounds contains the
circuit with $R_0$ rounds as its first layers (the start offsets do not depend on $R$),
and the extra gates there are later instances of the depositing checks, whose CNOTs move no
data error. If instance $R_0$ of every reading check reads after the last layer of the
$R_0$-round circuit, an undetected logical of that circuit stays undetected with the same
logical effect at every $R\ge R_0$: the data error after the last fault is unchanged, every
later comparison sees the same syndrome, and the final readout agrees. This condition is
independent of $R$ and holds for $g_8$, $g_9$, $b_9$, $b_{10}$ and IBM's circuits, so their
$R=1$ witnesses of weight 11, 11, 9, 9, 10 and 8 bound $\dcirc$ for every $R$; for $g_{10}$,
$u_{11}$ and $c_{11}$ a weight-$d$ data logical applied after initialisation
suffices\cl{L-4,L-23}. More generally, for translation-invariant schedules in which all
checks of each type share one preparation time, the detector error model of $R$ rounds
embeds into that of $R+1$ rounds, in place or delayed by one period, so the circuit distance
is non-increasing in $R$; we know of no schedule whose circuit distance increases with $R$.

\emph{Period lemma.} A signature is realisable at period $P$ iff the integer system
$x_{\pi(i+1)}-x_{\pi(i)}\ge1$, $x_{\pi(6)}-x_{\pi(1)}\le P-3$ for both orders and
$G(k,l)P+1\le t_X(A_k)-t_Z(B_l)\le G(k,l)P+P-1$, likewise on the right qubit ($G$ equals
$\delta$ up to a constant, SM~\ref{sm:class}), has an integer solution, i.e.\ its constraint
graph has no positive cycle. For $u_{11}$ the longest chain through a check window has
eight steps, so its minimum period is $8+3=11$; the same holds for $c_{11}$, and neither
signature occurs at $P=8$, 9 or 10\cl{L-16}.

\section{Notation and noise model}\label{sm:notation}
\emph{Codes.} The gross code~\cite{Bravyi2024} has $\ell=12$, $m=6$, $A=x^3+y+y^2$,
$B=y^3+x+x^2$, $H_X=[A\,|\,B]$, $H_Z=[B^{T}\,|\,A^{T}]$; qubits and checks are indexed by
$i=6a+b$ for the monomial $x^ay^b$, with left and right blocks of 72 qubits. Term labels:
$A_1=x^3$, $A_2=y$, $A_3=y^2$, $B_1=y^3$, $B_2=x$, $B_3=x^2$. The $X$ check at $(x,y)$ acts
on left qubit $(x,y)+A_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)+B_k$; the $Z$ check at $(x,y)$ on left
qubit $(x,y)-B_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)-A_k$. $\code{90,8,10}$ has $\ell=15$, $m=3$,
$A=x^9+y+y^2$, $B=1+x^2+x^7$~\cite{Bravyi2024}, labels $A_1=x^9$, $A_2=y$, $A_3=y^2$,
$B_1=1$, $B_2=x^2$, $B_3=x^7$, index $i=3a+b$; an exhaustive search finds no logical of
weight $\le9$, so $d=10$.

\emph{Circuits.} In every round each ancilla is reset, applies its six CNOTs (ancilla as
control for $X$ checks, as target for $Z$ checks) and is measured. A $Z$-basis memory
initialises the data in $|0\rangle$, runs $R$ rounds and reads all data in the $Z$ basis;
detectors are the first measurement of each $Z$ check, consecutive comparisons, and the
comparison of the last measurement with the final readout. The $X$ basis is analogous.

\emph{Noise and $\dcirc$.} SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}: a two-qubit depolarizing channel
after every CNOT, a flip after every reset and before every measurement, a single-qubit
depolarizing channel on every qubit idle in a time step. A fault is one Pauli component of
one channel; faults with identical detector and observable effects form one mechanism, and
$\dcirc$ counts mechanisms. In a $Z$-basis memory only $Z$-check detectors exist, so only
the $X$ part of each fault matters. $\dcirc$ of a circuit is the smaller of the values of
the two bases; all circuits we construct reach it in both. In the logical-error-rate
studies the idle channel is rescaled, $p_{\rm idle}=\lambda p$.

\section{The staircase model}\label{sm:model}\cl{L-24}
Take a $Z$-basis memory. Writers are $X$ checks: an $X$ error on the ancilla after its
$(k-1)$-th CNOT is deposited on $q_k,\dots,q_6$ at the times of their CNOTs. Readers are $Z$
checks; instance $t$ of $z$ reads $q$ at $\rho_z(q)+tP$. The index of a deposit at time $u$
is $\sigma_z(q,u)=\min\{t:\rho_z(q)+tP>u\}$. Measurement $m_t$ of $z$ equals the parity of
deposits with index $\le t$ plus its own readout flip, so a deposit of index $s$ flips exactly
detector $m_s+m_{s-1}$ (the final detector for $s=R$), and a readout flip of instance $t$
acts as the two indices $\{t,t+1\}$. A fault set is undetected iff every check receives each
index an even number of times; its logical effect is the $L$ image of the remaining data
error. Every SD6 fault component is one model fault (Table~\ref{tab:model}); conversely every
model fault is an SD6 fault, since every data qubit idles or takes part in a CNOT in every
layer. For all circuits used here the set of model signatures equals the set of detector
signatures of the SD6 detector error model for $R=1$, 2 (and 3 where checked), in both bases.

\emph{Images and light logicals.} Each fault's data image is zero, one data column, or one

an extended logical (End Matter). For all circuits used, the zero-image mechanisms are
exactly the readout flips: no hook residual with $k=2,\dots,6$ has zero image.

\section{The class $\T(P)$}\label{sm:class}\cl{L-1}
\emph{Overlaps.} For both codes the nine sums $A_i+B_j$ are distinct, so $X$ check $c$
overlaps exactly the nine $Z$ checks $c+A_i+B_j$, each on two qubits: the left qubit
$c+A_i$ (term $A_i$ for the $X$ check, $B_j$ for the $Z$ check) and the right qubit $c+B_j$
(term $B_j$ for $X$, $A_i$ for $Z$); 648 overlapping pairs for the gross code and 405 for
$\code{90,8,10}$.

\emph{Determinism.} Let $G_L(i,j)=\lfloor(t_X(A_i)-t_Z(B_j))/P\rfloor$ and
$G_R(i,j)=\lfloor(t_X(B_j)-t_Z(A_i))/P\rfloor$. $X$ instance $r$ acts on the left shared
qubit before $Z$ instance $s$ iff $s-r>G_L$, and likewise on the right. The measured operator
is the check iff the number of shared qubits on which $X$ acts first is even for every
$s-r$, i.e.\ $G_L=G_R=:G$. Schedules violating this produce non-deterministic detectors in
the simulator. A hook of instance $r$ is seen by the $Z$ check $c+A_i+B_j$ at index
$r+G(i,j)$ plus a constant fixed by the instance labelling, so $\delta$ and $G$ differ by a
constant; flatness and costs are the same for both.

\emph{Signatures.} The bulk fault model of a circuit in $\T(P)$ depends only on its
signature $(\pi_X,\pi_Z,\delta)$: data errors depend on the read order of the three $Z$
checks of a qubit, hooks on the suffixes of $\pi_X$ and on $\delta$, and readout flips on
nothing. Canonical form: $\min t_X=0$ and $0\le t_X(A_1)-t_Z(B_1)\le P-1$; every legal
schedule has exactly one canonical timing vector, by a global time shift and a relabelling
of $Z$ instances.

\section{The criterion in practice}\label{sm:criterion}\cl{L-13,L-14}
\emph{Window conditions.} They reduce to $R$-independent inequalities, since the start
offsets of the memory circuit do not depend on $R$. They hold, with finite and bulk indices
equal for every deposit, for all circuits of Table~I (checked for $R=1,2,3$ for every
circuit, and up to $R=12$ or 20 for most). They fail for most random legal
schedules (61 of 80 schedule-basis pairs in a random sample), so they must be checked per
circuit; we know no example in which their failure creates additional finite-$R$ lifts.

\emph{Solvers.} Bulk realisability was decided by a CP-SAT model (one integer shift per
realisation, perfect matchings of equal indices at each check, readout flips as two extra
items) and by a Bellman--Ford solver of the difference system with pairing branches, each
written independently; every reported realisation was re-checked by counting index
parities. Positive controls: non-interleaved controls with the same orders realise all
listed logicals; $u_{16}$, an interleaved $\code{90,8,10}$ circuit with $\dcirc=9$,
realises 315 of its weight-9 logicals in both bases.

\emph{Short certificates.} Two-hook (or longer hook-only) cycles certify all 144 weight-10
logicals of $u_{11}$ (holonomy 2), 5,400 of its 7,344 of weight 11, and 405 of the 585
weight-8 logicals of $c_{11}$; the rest need data slack or pairing branches. For $g_{10}$
only 1,224 of 3,204 weight-10 logicals have a two-hook certificate, all with holonomy 1,
and none of the 432 weight-9 logicals of $g_8$: their non-realisability rests on exact
solving.

\section{Period 8}\label{sm:p8}\cl{L-12}
\emph{Pair table.} For both codes and both check types, each of the 15 pairs of term labels
lies in a weight-$d$ logical (witnesses verified by linear algebra, 60 of 60). For triples,
8 ($\code{90,8,10}$) and 10 (gross) of the 20 lie in weight-$d$ logicals.

\emph{Flat pair hooks.} The pair hooks of an order $\pi$ are the last pair
$S_2=\{\pi_5,\pi_6\}$ and the residual of length 4, which equals the first pair
$\{\pi_1,\pi_2\}$ modulo the check. Among the 17,424 signatures of $\T(8)$, every one has at

after their last read, hook instance between $-4$ and 4) and verified it in the bulk model;
sampled witnesses were replayed in the memory circuit by stim.

\section{Periods 9 and 10}\label{sm:p910}\cl{L-2,L-15}
\emph{Patterns.} A pattern is a set of data errors (residual qubits with read-order cuts),
hooks (check type, suffix, round offset) and readout flips, together with the timing
conditions under which it is undetected in the bulk model. The five results use 482
($\code{90,8,10}$, $P=8$, weight $\le8$), 856 ($P=9$, $\le9$), 1,718 ($P=10$, $\le9$), 599
(gross, $P=8$, $\le11$) and 1,186 (gross, $P=9$, $\le11$) patterns. Every pattern's residual
data error lies in $\ker H$ and outside the row space of the opposite check matrix, i.e.\ is
a nontrivial logical (4,841 of 4,841).

\emph{Certificates.} For each result a CNF encodes legality (one-hot steps, distinctness,
canonical form, span, data-qubit distinctness modulo $P$, determinism) and one clause per
pattern forbidding the timings under which it applies. Each of the five formulas is
unsatisfiable; CaDiCaL produces DRAT proofs verified by drat-trim. Only time shift and
$Z$-instance relabelling are used as symmetry reductions; neither changes the bulk physics.

\emph{SAT-free check.} Independently, every canonical legal timing vector (all 10,271,304
at $P=10$) was tested against the patterns with a separately written bulk model: none is
uncovered. The bulk model agrees with stim on 240 of 240 random fault sets, and every pattern
used was injected into the memory circuit on the vectors it covers (none of 10,293 injections
failed; removing or shifting one fault always triggered a detector).

\emph{Small $R$.} The results are bulk statements. For 25 random vectors per result the
covering pattern is already undetected, after a global instance shift, in the memory
circuit with $R=1$ or 2, and remains so for the next three $R$.

\emph{Direct search.} The search works on the detector error model of the simulator, not on
the extended code: it branches on the mechanisms covering an odd detector, excludes earlier
siblings, splits root cases by translation orbits (the observable action of each
translation is solved and verified on all mechanisms) and closes the last levels with a
meet-in-the-middle table. A minimum-weight logical has no proper undetected subset, so the
absence of solutions up to weight $W$ means $\dcirc>W$ at that $R$. It also finds the $R=1$

\emph{Setup.} All circuits are noised by the same SD6 implementation with $p_{\rm
idle}=\lambda p$; both bases are sampled separately and added. BP-OSD (min-sum, 10,000
iterations, OSD-CS order 7) and Relay-BP use identical parameters and exact priors.

\emph{Three tiers.} (i) Exact, without statistical error: per round, IBM's gross circuit has

information only: the undetected terms they give lie many orders of magnitude below the
measured rates. (ii) Measured, by direct sampling or by stratified sampling at fixed fault

$\lambda=1$, $p=2\times10^{-3}$, BP-OSD, $g_{10}$ and $u_{11}$ have per-round rates

\section{Logical error rates in a neutral-atom noise model}\label{sm:na}\cl{L-19}
\emph{Architecture and timing.} Data atoms stay in the entangling zone; the $X$- and
$Z$-ancilla arrays are held in movable tweezers and translated rigidly, and each translation
is followed by a global Rydberg CZ pulse and local single-qubit gates (CX $=$
H$\cdot$CZ$\cdot$H on the target). One move serves one pair (check type, polynomial term).
Both codes have three terms in each of $A$ and $B$, so every translation-invariant schedule
needs exactly 12 moves per round; this holds for the period-11 circuits $u_{11}$ and
$c_{11}$ studied here and for IBM's depth-8 circuits, which also have two mid-circuit
readout layers per round. In the main model the two ancilla arrays move one after the
other, as in qSIEVE~\cite{Viszlai2026}, and each move takes a fixed time. Equal move counts
do not imply equal durations: with a move time growing as the square root of the distance,
the summed path is 6\% longer for $u_{11}$ and 21\% longer for $c_{11}$ (layout dependent);
at current parameters this changes the fault mass by less than 2\%.

readout the unmeasured atoms receive the scattering error of Table~\ref{tab:na};
preparation, gates, measurement and the two ancilla transfers per round carry the errors of
Table~\ref{tab:na}. The set ``cur$\times$0.5'' halves every error probability of ``cur''; it
is a uniform scaling, not a literature value.

\section{Earlier constructions}\label{sm:earlier}\cl{L-22}
\emph{Non-interleaved bound for $\code{90,8,10}$.} Up to swaps of positions 1,2 and 5,6
and reversal, a check of this code has 90 order types; for 36 of them the extended code of
that check alone has a logical of weight $\le8$. For the remaining 54, any realised residual
columns inside one of the 558 weight-10 $X$-type logicals whose total size reduction is at
least 2 give an extended logical of weight $\le8$. A SAT encoding of the choice of one type
per check with one clause per such combination (17,910 combinations, each verified) is
unsatisfiable, with a DRAT proof verified by drat-trim. Hence every assignment of orders has
$\dext\le8$ on the $X$-check side and every non-interleaved circuit has $\dcirc\le8$ by SBB's
Cor.~1.

\emph{Longer periods.} Before the class $\T$ was analysed, full distance was reached at
longer periods: for $\code{90,8,10}$ by a translation-invariant period-16 circuit with three
CNOT-free steps (orders $A_2A_1A_3B_2B_3B_1$ for $X$ checks and $B_1B_3B_2A_3A_1A_2$ for $Z$
checks, with the extended code of $c_{11}$) and a non-wrapping period-12 circuit; for the gross
code by colour-symmetric circuits of periods 18 and 21 whose orders lie in the class of SBB's
Fig.~12, and therefore share its extended code ($\dext=11$, 4,224 weight-11 logicals per

\emph{SBB's Fig.~12.} It is non-interleaved, so SBB's Prop.~3 and Cor.~1 give
$\dcirc=\dext$ for every $R$, and two independent exhaustive searches give $\dext=11$; hence
$\dcirc=11$, as SBB conjectured. It is colour-tiled rather than translation invariant and
therefore not in $\T(8)$. Its orders cannot reach 12 by retiming within several symmetric
families (bulk statements, each certified by DRAT); periodic schedules of period 8 keeping
its orders are, up to translation, four non-interleaved circuits, each with weight-11
undetected errors for $R=1,\dots,6$.

\section{Verification and data}\label{sm:data}
All circuits were built and simulated with Stim~\cite{Gidney2021stim}; feasibility
questions were decided with CP-SAT, CDCL SAT solvers and a Bellman--Ford solver, and
extended-code searches use dedicated depth-first and meet-in-the-middle searches. Every
result was reproduced by a separately written implementation: the class enumeration, the
bulk model and its agreement with the detector error model, the pattern coverage, the
lists, the bulk lifts and the direct searches. The data set contains the circuit
specifications, the lists of extended logicals, the impossibility patterns, the upper-bound
witnesses, the certificates (formulas, proofs up to 100~MB, and SHA-256 hashes with
regeneration scripts for larger ones) and the reproduction scripts; it is available at
[Zenodo DOI].
