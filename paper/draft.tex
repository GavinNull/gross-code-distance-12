\title{A single-ancilla syndrome circuit with circuit distance 12 for the gross code}

\author{\todo{author list -- PI decision}}
\affiliation{\todo{affiliations}}

\date{Internal draft, \today}

\begin{abstract}
Strikis, Browne and Beverland proved that no non-interleaved single-ancilla
syndrome-extraction circuit for the gross code $\code{144,12,12}$ reaches circuit
distance 12, and asked whether any interleaved circuit can. We give one. Each
check's CNOT order is obtained from the order of their distance-11 circuit by
in-pair swaps and reversal, operations that leave the extended code (and hence
its distance 11) unchanged, while $X$- and $Z$-check CNOTs are interleaved in time.
Under standard circuit-level depolarizing noise (SD6) we prove $\dcirc=12$ in both
memory bases for any number of rounds: the extended code excludes weight
$\le 10$; none of the 4224 weight-11 extended-code logicals per basis lifts to an
undetected circuit error; and a split-and-translate argument extends the
finite-round computation to all rounds. The price is time: the shortest instance
has a period of 21 steps per round, against 8 for the earlier circuits; whether
period 8 suffices is open, and we make no claim about logical error rates. We also
show why the problem was hard (retimings of the original orders, and all 944
colour-symmetric period-8 circuits in the same extended-code class, have weight-11
undetected errors; random circuits from our search family never certified), prove
the conjectured $\dcirc=11$ of the original circuit, and give exact circuit
distances for 80 morphing circuits, 8 of which fall below their end-cycle
distance, as evidence that circuit distances must be proven rather than inferred.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Introduction.}
Bivariate-bicycle (BB) codes~\cite{Bravyi2024} are leading candidates for
low-overhead quantum memory, and the gross code $\code{144,12,12}$ is the flagship of
IBM's architecture~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}. What a syndrome circuit delivers,
however, is not the code distance $d$ but the circuit-level distance $\dcirc$: the
minimum number of circuit faults that flip a logical observable without
triggering any detector. For the gross code no circuit had been shown to have
$\dcirc=d$. The IBM circuit (8 time steps per round) has $\dcirc\le10$~\cite{Bravyi2024,TourDeGross}.
Strikis, Browne and Beverland (SBB)~\cite{Strikis2026} proved that every
\emph{non-interleaved} single-ancilla circuit has $\dcirc\le11$ (their Prop.~4,
which carries no depth assumption), gave a depth-8 left-right circuit (their
Fig.~12) conjectured to reach 11, and stated as open (Sec.~IV.3)
``whether any interleaved circuit can achieve circuit distance 12''~\cite{Strikis2026}.

We answer this question in the affirmative\cl{40}. Table~\ref{tab:p21} specifies
a single-ancilla, CNOT-based, interleaved circuit for the gross code itself; we prove
$\dcirc=12$ for it under SD6 noise in both memory bases and for every number of
rounds\cl{31}. The question as posed carries no depth condition, and our circuit
pays for its distance in time: its period is 21 steps per round, against 8 for
the circuits of Refs.~\cite{Bravyi2024,Strikis2026}\cl{37}. Whether $\dcirc=12$ is
attainable at depth 8 remains open, and we evaluate only distance, not the logical
error rate at finite $p$.

The second half of the Letter explains why this took more than a search. We prove
SBB's conjecture that Fig.~12 has $\dcirc=11$\cl{10}, show that retimings which
keep its CNOT orders are obstructed in every family we could decide\cl{14,15,16}, and that period-8 colour-symmetric
circuits in the new class are too\cl{38}. Finally, exact results for 80 morphing
circuits~\cite{ShawTerhal2025}, including $\dcirc=12$ for a different
$\code{144,12,12}$ code\cl{1}, show that circuit distances inferred from code
properties or unrefuted upper bounds can be wrong\cl{5,6}.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{Setting.}
We consider memory experiments: data initialisation, $R$ rounds of syndrome
extraction, and transversal data readout, in both the $Z$ and $X$ basis.
Except for the morphing circuits discussed later, every check has one dedicated
ancilla, reset, coupled by six CNOTs and measured once per round. Faults are single-operation Pauli components of standard
circuit-level depolarizing noise (SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}: two-qubit
depolarization after each CNOT, flips after resets and before measurements,
single-qubit depolarization on idle qubits), counted as distinct
detector-error-model (DEM) mechanisms~\cite{Gidney2021stim}. $\dcirc$ is the
minimum number of such faults whose combined effect triggers no detector and flips
a logical observable; we report it per basis, and in every case below the two
bases agree. All lower bounds also hold for any noise model whose faults are a
subset of these (e.g.\ no idle noise). Correlated multi-operation faults,
leakage, biased noise and CZ-based compilations are outside scope. Upper bounds are
realised by explicit witnesses injected with probability one into the noiseless
\texttt{stim} circuit (zero detectors fired, an observable flipped).

\emph{Extended code.} For single-ancilla CNOT circuits SBB append to the
parity-check matrix one column per ``residual'' (a proper suffix of a check's CNOT
order, which an ancilla fault after the $k$-th CNOT spreads onto the data), giving
an extended code of distance $\dext$; every single fault, propagated to the end
of the circuit, has a data image equal to zero or to one extended-code column
(their Table~I). For the gross code with the orders of Fig.~12, $\dext=11$ in
both bases\cl{11}.

\paragraph{Construction.}
Two facts constrain the design. First, by Prop.~4 of SBB the circuit must be
interleaved. Second, the CNOT orders of Fig.~12 are a dead end as far as we can decide: every one of the
4224 weight-11 extended logicals per basis lifts to an undetected circuit error of
Fig.~12 itself\cl{13}, and in every family of retimings of the Fig.~12 orders we
could decide, a ``ten data faults plus one hook'' weight-11 error survives
(below and Table~\ref{tab:gross})\cl{14,15,16}. Changing orders freely, on the other hand,
risks lowering $\dext$ below 11, after which no lift argument can certify 12.

The way out is that the extended code depends on far less than the order. A
residual of length 5 equals the first qubit up to the check itself, length 4 the
first two, length 3 the complement of the first three, and length 2 the last two.
The extended code is therefore fixed by three unordered sets per check: the
first two, the first three and the last two qubits. Swapping positions 1 and 2,
swapping positions 5 and 6, and reversing the order preserve these sets, so all
eight orders they generate share the extended code of Fig.~12; we verified
column-by-column equality of the extended codes for the circuits below\cl{32}.
Within this class, $\dext=11$ and the complete list of 4224 weight-11 extended
logicals per basis carry over unchanged, but the times at which residuals are
deposited on the data, relative to the other checks' readouts, are different, and
these times decide whether an extended logical lifts.

We searched colour-symmetric circuits in which all ancillas are reset and measured
together (a non-wrapping frame), choosing per colour class one of the eight orders
and the CNOT times with a SAT solver, subject to legality, determinism, and
constraints that block every modelled weight-11 extended logical in a bulk
Pauli-frame model (SM~\ref{sm:gross12}); model solutions were compressed in time
and then certified exactly. Table~\ref{tab:p21} and Fig.~\ref{fig:timing} show the
shortest certified instance. It is interleaved (48 data qubits switch four times between $X$- and
$Z$-type CNOTs per period), legal and deterministic, measures the gross code
itself, and, as it happens, needs only in-pair swaps; 24 of its 144 checks keep
their Fig.~12 order\cl{31,32}. Two further instances, of period 38 and 26 (the latter
obtained by compressing the former), have the same certified distance\cl{37}.

\paragraph{Proof of $\dcirc=12$.}
\emph{Weight $\le10$.} Augment the circuit with one observable per row of the
extended code's check matrix $H$ and logical matrix $L$, evaluated on the final
data readout. In our circuit template, the augmented observable of row $H_i$ equals
the sum of all detectors of check $i$ (first round, consecutive-round
comparisons, final readout). Hence for any undetected fault set the $H$ parts of
its images sum to zero and its logical effect is the $L$ part, so its nonzero
images form an extended-code logical of weight at most the number of faults.
This uses only this template identity and SBB's Table~I property, not their
Prop.~3, and is therefore insensitive to interleaving. Both ingredients were
checked on the DEM, in both bases, for every $R$ used below (up to $R=20$), without
exception; the image set equals the 360 extended columns\cl{33}. With $\dext=11$, no undetected logical
of weight $\le10$ exists.

\emph{Weight 11.} A weight-11 undetected logical must map to 11 distinct columns
forming one of the 4224 weight-11 extended logicals of its basis. For each of them
we decide exactly whether one DEM term per column can be chosen with zero detector
sum (CP-SAT with exactly-one and XOR constraints; and, without a solver, a
meet-in-the-middle enumeration). None lifts: 0/4224 in both bases for
$R=1,\dots,14$ and $16$ (and up to $R=20$ for the period-38 instance; the
solver-free check for $R\le3$)\cl{34}. The same programs lift 4224/4224 for
Fig.~12 (positive control)\cl{34}.

\emph{All $R$.} All ancillas share reset and measurement layers, so rounds are
cleanly separated, and the noisy circuit is periodic in time (verified layer by
layer). If a weight-11 undetected logical had a fault-free round strictly inside
its time span, cutting there would split it into two undetected parts whose
observables add, one of them an undetected logical of weight $\le10$, which is
impossible. A minimal logical therefore spans at most 11 consecutive rounds, and by
periodicity it translates into a circuit with at most 13 rounds (one clean round
kept on each side), which the exact runs exclude. Thus no weight-11 undetected
logical exists for any $R$\cl{35}.

\emph{Upper bound.} A weight-12 data logical injected after the data reset fires
no detector and flips the observable ($R=1,3,6,10$, both bases), so
$\dcirc=12$\cl{36}. A heuristic minimum-weight search (truncated graph search,
BP-OSD and local search) finds nothing below 12 either\cl{36}.

The lower bound rests on two earlier audited computations, $\dext=11$ for the
Fig.~12 class\cl{11} and the completeness of the 4224-element list\cl{13}; a direct
solve of the full DEM without the lift reduction (MaxSAT and CP-SAT, $R=1$, $Z$ basis) did not
terminate within 1--2 hours and is not part of the proof\cl{37}.

\paragraph{Cost and scope.}
The circuit uses the same qubits as the IBM and SBB circuits (one ancilla per
check) but 21 time steps per round instead of 8, i.e.\ about 2.6 times as many
steps and correspondingly more idle locations\cl{37}. $\dcirc$ counts faults and is
blind to this cost, so we claim only that the circuit reaches distance 12 and make
no statement about its performance: its logical error rate at finite $p$ has not
been evaluated.
The result holds for the SD6 fault set and the CNOT template above; CZ-native
compilations and other noise models were not examined\cl{37}. Within the same
extended-code class, the period-8 colour-symmetric circuits are completely
classified: there are 944 legal deterministic ones (two independent enumerations),
and each has a weight-11 undetected logical already at $R=1$\cl{38}. Per-check-free
period-8 circuits, and periods 9--20, are not decided.

\emph{Classical control.} In the same family (colour-symmetric, non-wrapping, orders
in the Fig.~12 class), 0 of 233 random legal deterministic circuits certify, whereas
the constraint-guided search produced the certified circuits\cl{39}. The distance
comes from the blocking reasoning, not from the class alone.

\emph{Relation to prior work.} To our knowledge no previous circuit for the gross
code itself, with or without additional ancillas, has been shown, numerically or
rigorously, to reach $\dcirc=12$\cl{40}. The morphing circuits of
Refs.~\cite{ShawTerhal2025,Wills2026}, which use the same number of qubits and are
believed to reach 12~\cite{Wills2026}, act on a different $\code{144,12,12}$ code and
have no dedicated ancillas (below). Nor is ours
the first BB circuit proven to reach the code distance: an exact value 6 for an
IBM-type $\code{72,12,6}$ circuit is in the public data of Ref.~\cite{Webster2026},
and we prove 6 for the IBM $\code{72,12,6}$ circuit (SM~\ref{sm:other})\cl{21}.
Nguyen, Rimbach-Russ and Bosco proposed lower-bounding interleaved circuits
via a non-interleaved counterpart combined with a single-round reduction, as
future work~\cite{Nguyen2026}\cl{29}; our lift argument is a different route.

\paragraph{Why the original orders cannot reach 12.}
For Fig.~12 itself we prove SBB's conjecture: $\dcirc=11$ for any $R$, both bases.
A complete symmetric depth-first search (DFS) on its extended code (360 merged
columns per side, a 24-element order-preserving translation symmetry, 15 orbits)
finds no logical of weight 10, reproduced on an independently constructed
instance and under a second symmetry group, and weight 11 is attained\cl{10,11}.
By Prop.~4 of SBB this is optimal among non-interleaved circuits\cl{12}.

Retiming the same orders does not help in any family we can decide. For periodic
schedules of period $P\le8$ the answer is exact: keeping Fig.~12's orders forces
$P=8$, a complete search finds 4 conflict-free circuits up to translation (Fig.~12
among them), and each has injection-verified weight-11 undetected errors for
$R=1,\dots,6$ in both bases\cl{14,15} (Fig.~\ref{fig:lift}). For longer periods, a bulk frame-model
analysis finds a weight-11 error of ten data faults and one hook in every legal
deterministic circuit of several symmetric and per-check families
(SM~\ref{sm:gross12}, Table~\ref{tab:gross})\cl{16,18}; other families remain
open\cl{17}. The new circuit escapes this by changing the order within the pairs
(and the timing) while keeping the pairs, and with them the extended code.

\paragraph{Circuit distance must be proven: morphing circuits.}
Morphing circuits~\cite{ShawTerhal2025} have no dedicated ancillas: each round
contracts half of the stabilisers of a ``mid-cycle'' code onto single qubits,
measures them, and re-expands, so that between rounds the state is encoded in an
``end-cycle'' code. Applied to the $\code{288,12,18}$ BB code with the homomorphism
$f_x$, this yields a 288-qubit circuit whose end-cycle code is a
$\code{144,12,12}$ code with weight-9 checks, reported with
$\dt_{\rm circ}\le12$~\cite{ShawTerhal2025}; Ref.~\cite{Wills2026} (App.~C)
describes a morphing idling circuit ``for the gross code'' believed, from
Monte-Carlo sampling, to reach 12. We prove $\dcirc=12$ for this circuit (public
layer order) for every number of rounds, both bases, noisy or ideal
boundaries\cl{1}, by a single-round reduction (Prop.~1 of
Ref.~\cite{ShawTerhal2026}, whose premise we verify in a basis-free form,
SM~\ref{sm:fold})\cl{3} and exhaustive search. This end-cycle code is not the gross
code: its minimum stabiliser weight is 9, versus 6\cl{2}; whether it is the circuit
of Ref.~\cite{Wills2026} is not stated there and we have not confirmed it. To our
knowledge this is the first rigorous $\dcirc$ lower bound for any morphing
circuit\cl{4}.

The same construction has more freedom than its tables suggest: ten (mother code,
homomorphism) pairs, two contraction variants and a free order of the terms
$(a_2,a_3)$, $(b_2,b_3)$ give 80 circuits, which we reconstructed gate by gate and
matched against an independent implementation. Their exact $\dcirc$: 72 equal the
end-cycle distance $\dt$, 8 fall below it (Fig.~\ref{fig:morph};
Table~\ref{tab:morph})\cl{5}. For $\code{108,8,10}\to\code{54,8,8}$
the four legal term orders give 6, 6, 7, 7 (7 for the public implementation's
order)\cl{6}: the caption of Table~IX of Ref.~\cite{ShawTerhal2025} states that the
order of the elements of $A$ and $B$ is not important for these schedules because
the end-cycle codes are invariant under $a_2\leftrightarrow a_3$,
$b_2\leftrightarrow b_3$; the codes are invariant, but the circuit distance is
not\cl{6}. For $f_x$ on the gross code the end-cycle code is $\code{72,12,8}$ but
$\dcirc=6$, showing the bound $\le6$ of that table to be tight\cl{7}. The sub-$\dt$
errors consist only of CNOT faults within a single interior half-cycle; they are
not boundary artefacts\cl{8}.

\paragraph{Discussion.}
The gross code admits a single-ancilla syndrome circuit that preserves its full
distance: the obstacle identified by SBB is specific to non-interleaved circuits,
not intrinsic to the single-ancilla model, although with the original CNOT orders
we find it again in every retiming family we could decide. Two
methodological points carry over. Keeping the extended code fixed while changing
the order turns an open-ended circuit search into a finite lift problem against a
precomputed list of minimum-weight extended logicals. And neither code-level
invariances nor unrefuted upper bounds determine $\dcirc$: the morphing family
contains circuits that fall short of their end-cycle distance in ways the code does
not reveal. The same tools give further proven distances for BB circuits, among
them $\dcirc=10$ for a depth-8 $\code{108,8,10}$ circuit (previously $\le8$) and
$\dcirc=6$ for the IBM $\code{72,12,6}$ circuit (SM~\ref{sm:other},
Table~\ref{tab:bbthm})\cl{19,21,22}.

\paragraph{Open questions.}
(i) Can $\dcirc=12$ be reached for the gross code at period 8, or anywhere between
9 and 20? Colour-symmetric period-8 circuits in the Fig.~12 class cannot; per-check-free
ones are undecided. (ii) Does the distance gain survive the extra idling, i.e.\ is
the logical error rate at accessible $p$ lower than for the depth-8 circuits? We
have not evaluated this. (iii) Do the proven distances survive correlated faults,
leakage, or CZ-native compilations? (iv) Is the IBM morphing idling circuit of
Ref.~\cite{Wills2026} the one we certify? (v) Three-round ($\mathbb{Z}_3$) morphing
circuits and non-Abelian morphing codes~\cite{ShawTerhal2025} are not covered here.

\section{Fault model and definition of $\dcirc$}\label{sm:noise}
Noise is inserted by a fixed routine (SD6~\cite{Gidney2021honeycomb}): after every
CNOT a two-qubit depolarizing channel; after every reset a flip; before every
measurement a flip; on every qubit not acted on in a time step a single-qubit
depolarizing channel (also in reset and measurement layers). A \emph{fault} is one
Pauli component of one such channel. Two faults with identical detector and
observable signatures are the same DEM mechanism; $\dcirc$ counts mechanisms.
For lower bounds of CSS circuits we use the CSS relaxation: in a $Z$-basis memory
we keep only $Z$-type detectors and the $X$ part of each fault (and vice versa).
This relaxation can only lower the minimum, so it is valid for lower bounds;
upper bounds are always checked on the full circuit with all detectors.
Lower bounds hold for any sub-model (e.g.\ no idle noise). For the morphing
circuits the witnesses of $\dcirc\le\dt$ use idle components after a reset layer
(or, in the first round, initial reset flips); for a no-idle model those upper
bounds rely on the noisy initial data reset and would need re-checking
otherwise\cl{28}.

\section{The gross-code circuit with $\dcirc=12$}\label{sm:p21}
\emph{Code and conventions.} Gross code~\cite{Bravyi2024}: $\ell=12$, $m=6$,
$A=x^3+y+y^2$, $B=y^3+x+x^2$, $H_X=[A\,|\,B]$, $H_Z=[B^{T}\,|\,A^{T}]$, qubit and check
index $i=6a+b$ for the monomial $x^ay^b$. Our check matrices are element-wise equal
to those of the benchmark implementation (not merely equivalent); $n=144$, $k=12$,
$d=12$~\cite{Bravyi2024}\cl{31}. Term labels: $A_1=x^3$, $A_2=y$, $A_3=y^2$,
$B_1=y^3$, $B_2=x$, $B_3=x^2$. The $X$ check at $(x,y)$ acts on left qubit
$(x,y)+A_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)+B_k$; the $Z$ check at $(x,y)$ on left qubit
$(x,y)-B_k$ and right qubit $(x,y)-A_k$. Colour of a check: $(x+y)\bmod 3$.
Table~\ref{tab:p21} was extracted from the schedule file
\texttt{search/experiments/e021/cand\_p21\_spec.json} by
\texttt{paper/scripts/extract\_class\_orders.py}, which asserts that this labelling
reproduces every check's support and that each (type, colour) class has a single
order and timing; the same script applied to the Fig.~12 schedule gives the
orders $X_0$: $B_1B_2B_3A_3A_2A_1$, $X_1$: $B_2B_3B_1A_3A_2A_1$, $X_2$:
$B_1B_2B_3A_2A_1A_3$, $Z_0$: $A_1A_3A_2B_3B_1B_2$, $Z_1$: $A_2A_1A_3B_1B_2B_3$,
$Z_2$: $A_2A_1A_3B_3B_1B_2$ (pipelined, period 8), from which the right column of
Table~\ref{tab:p21} follows\cl{32}.

\emph{Legality.} 72+72 checks, each with exactly six CNOTs on its support at
distinct times inside its (reset, measure) window; no data qubit in two CNOTs at
one time step; all ancillas reset at step 0 and measured at step 20 of each period
(non-wrapping frame); the noiseless circuit fires no detector (64 shots) and the
DEM builds without non-determinism for $R=1,2,3,6$; the template has one dedicated
ancilla per check, reset--six CNOTs--measure per round\cl{31}. Interleaving: over
one period, 48 data qubits switch four times (cyclically) between $X$- and $Z$-type
CNOTs; Fig.~12 is non-interleaved\cl{31}.

\emph{Extended-code invariance.} In a $Z$-basis memory only $X$ errors matter.
An $X$ error on the ancilla of an $X$ check after its $k$-th CNOT reaches the suffix
$\{q_{k+1},\dots,q_6\}$; $Z$-check CNOTs (data$\to$ancilla) do not change the data
$X$ frame. So each fault's final data $X$ frame is zero, one data qubit, or a proper
suffix of an $X$-check order, and its column is $(H_Zs,L_Zs)$. Modulo the check
itself, a suffix of length 5 equals $\{q_1\}$, length 4 equals $\{q_1,q_2\}$,
length 3 equals the complement of $\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, and length 2 is $\{q_5,q_6\}$.
The column set is therefore determined by the three sets $\{q_1,q_2\}$,
$\{q_1,q_2,q_3\}$, $\{q_5,q_6\}$ per check; reversal maps them to
$\{q_5,q_6\}$, the last three, $\{q_1,q_2\}$, which give the same columns, and the
swaps $s_{12}$, $s_{56}$ leave them unchanged. The $X$ basis is symmetric. Computed
check: both sides have 360 merged columns, and the key sets are equal to those of
Fig.~12, for all three certified circuits\cl{32}.

\emph{Lift reduction (template identity + Table~I).} We add one observable per row
of $H$ and of $L$ on the final data readout. (i) In the circuit template the
augmented observable of row $H_i$ equals the sum of all detectors of check $i$
(first round, consecutive-round comparisons, final readout), so for an undetected
fault set the $H$ parts of the images sum to zero and the logical effect equals the
$L$ part. (ii) Every nonzero image is one extended-code column (SBB Table~I). Hence a
weight-$w$ undetected logical yields an extended logical of weight $\le w$; with
$\dext=11$, $w\ge11$, and for $w=11$ the eleven images are nonzero, distinct and form
one of the listed weight-11 extended logicals. The original DEM's terms are unions
of augmented terms, so the bound transfers. Prop.~3 of SBB (and its single-ancilla
propagation argument) is not used; interleaving is irrelevant to (i) and (ii).
Checked on the DEM: every term's per-check detector parity equals the $H$ part of
its image, observable equals $L$ part, and the image set equals the 360 columns, for
every $R$ used (up to $R=20$), both bases. In a $Z$-basis memory the template has no
$X$-check detectors; fewer detectors can only enlarge the set of undetected fault
sets, so the lower bound also holds with all detectors\cl{33}.

\emph{Exact lift.} Input: the 4224 weight-11 extended logicals per basis from the
independent DFS of the E-018 audit, expanded by the 24-element group\cl{13}.
Method 1: for each logical, CP-SAT decides whether one DEM term per column can be
chosen (exactly-one constraints) with every detector XOR zero. Method 2 (no
solver): split the 11 columns into two halves, enumerate all XORs of each half, and
intersect. Positive control: Fig.~12 lifts 4224/4224 ($Z$: $R=1$; $X$: $R=1,2$; both
methods). Period-21 circuit: 0/4224 for $R=1,\dots,14,16$, both bases (method 1);
0/4224 for $R=1,2,3$, both bases (method 2). Period-38 circuit: 0/4224 for
$R=1,\dots,14,16,20$ (method 1) and $R\le3$ (method 2). Period-26 circuit: 0/4224 for
$R=1,\dots,14$\cl{34}. The experimenter's independent lift program agrees where
both ran.

\emph{All $R$.} Premises (machine-checked for each circuit): in round $k$ all 144
ancillas are reset in layer $Pk$ and measured in layer $Pk+P-1$, with all CNOTs
strictly between, so the windows $[Pk,Pk+P-1]$ tile time; the noisy circuit is
layer-periodic (layer $t$ equals layer $t+P$ for rounds $1..12$; the $(R+1)$-round
circuit equals the $R$-round circuit with one block inserted); detectors are the
first-round, consecutive-round and final comparisons, identified by (check, round)
coordinates. (1) By the reduction above, weight $\le10$ is excluded for every $R$.
(2) Split lemma: let $F$ be a weight-11 undetected logical with a fault-free round
$r$ strictly inside its time span; let $F_<$ be its faults before $r$. With only
$F_<$, the data frame from round $r$ on is a constant $e$, every later round
measures $He$, so all comparison detectors from $r$ on and the final detectors
vanish; with only $F_>$, measurements up to round $r$ are unaffected. By linearity
both parts are undetected and their observables add to a nonzero value, so one of
them is an undetected logical of weight $\le10$, a contradiction. Hence $F$ occupies
at most 11 consecutive rounds $[a,b]$. (3) Translation: keep one clean round before
$a$ (if $a>0$) and one after $b$ (if $b<R-1$); the detectors touched by $F$ depend
only on layers in $[a-1,b+1]$, which periodicity makes identical in an $R'$-round
circuit with $R'\le13$. Since $R\le13$ is excluded exactly, no $R$ admits a weight-11
undetected logical. An alternative ``round deletion'' version (experimenter) needs
$R'\le14$; both were checked by the auditor and both ranges are covered\cl{35}.

\emph{Upper bound and heuristic attack.} Weight-12 data logicals found by CP-SAT
were injected as flips after the data reset: for $R=1,3,6,10$, both bases, zero
detectors fire and the observable flips; removing any one qubit makes the error
detectable. A heuristic minimum-weight search (truncated graph search, 400 BP-OSD
trials, local search; fresh seeds) returned 12 as its best value for
$R=1,2,3,7,10$ (period 38) and $R=1,3,7$ (period 21), both bases\cl{36}.

\emph{Not done.} A direct minimum-weight solve of the full DEM without the lift
reduction: MaxSAT timed out at 7200 s, CP-SAT ($\ge12$ target) returned UNKNOWN at
3600 s ($R=1$, $Z$). The $\dext=11$ computation was reproduced under two symmetry
reductions but not without symmetry\cl{11,37}.

\emph{Period-8 colour-symmetric circuits in the Fig.~12 class.} At $P=8$ the offsets
are forced to $1..6$ and measurement to 7; each (type, colour) class chooses one
of the eight orders and a start $s\in\mathbb{Z}_8$ (one start fixed by translation).
Legality: per data qubit, CNOT times distinct mod 8. Determinism: for every
overlapping $X/Z$ pair and instance offset, the number of shared qubits on which
the $X$ instance acts before the $Z$ instance is even. Two independent enumerations
(SAT; backtracking) give the same 944 circuits (hash-identical). All 944 are legal,
deterministic and share Fig.~12's extended code; an exact lift at $R=1$, $Z$ basis,
finds a weight-11 undetected logical in each (944/944), each witness rechecked as 11
distinct DEM terms with empty detector sum and nonzero $L$ part. Per-check-free $P=8$
circuits were not enumerated\cl{38}.

\emph{How the circuit was found, and the classical control.} Design (experimenter):
a SAT model over colour-symmetric non-wrapping circuits whose variables are a total
order of the $36$ (class, label) CNOT times and, per class, one of the eight orders;
constraints: legality, determinism, and for each modelled weight-11 extended logical
(176 orbit representatives per side, with multi-hook columns modelled by pinning the
smaller residual at its CNOT time; 168 representatives with overlapping supports
were left to the exact check) a clause that some detector separates it in the bulk
frame model. With the Fig.~12 orders pinned the model is UNSAT, consistent with the
obstruction of SM~\ref{sm:gross12}; with the class free it returned a candidate after
98 lazy rounds (4.1 s), which became the period-38 circuit after exact
certification. Compression of the dependency DAG gave period 26; enumerating 300
model candidates (40 s) and compressing each gave periods 21--29; of the 40 shortest,
39 were lifted at $R=1,\dots,6$ and all 39 certified (two of period 21; the 40th was
not run). Control in the same family: (a) uniformly random orders and interleavings with
rejection: $6\times10^7$ samples in 25 min gave 41 deterministic circuits, each
with at least 68 of the 184 modelled logicals liftable; (b) the same incremental SAT sampler without the logical clauses: 2277
legal deterministic circuits in 9 min, none with zero modelled liftable logicals
(minimum 25 of 184), and exact lifting of a random 10\% (233) certified 0. Only the 0/233 control is audited (confirmed-results ledger). The 39/39 count, the
4 s / 40 s timings and control (a) are unaudited observations from the experiment
log and are not used in the main text\cl{39}.

\section{Extended code, Prop.~3, and the exhaustive search}\label{sm:dfs}
\emph{Extended code.} For a single-ancilla circuit in which check $c$ applies its
CNOTs in the order $i_1,\dots,i_w$, the residuals are the suffixes
$\{i_l,\dots,i_w\}$, $l=2,\dots,w$. The $Z$-type extended code appends each $Z$-check
residual (as the sum of its data columns) to $(H_X,L_X)$; analogously for $X$.
Zero columns are dropped and identical $(H,L)$ columns merged.
Prop.~3 of Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026}: $\dcirc\ge\dext$ for any single-ancilla CNOT
circuit (interleaved or not); the converse $\dcirc\le\dext$ (their Cor.~1) needs
non-interleaving and is not used for our lower bounds. The key step---each single
fault, propagated to the end of the circuit, has a data image equal to zero or to
exactly one extended-code column---was checked on the evaluated circuits:
$\code{108,8,10}$ ($R=3$, 16\,416 fault components per basis), gross Fig.~12
($R=1,2,3$), IBM $\code{72,12,6}$ ($R=1,\dots,10$) and the four LRC theorems:
0 exceptions, all columns realised\cl{27}.

\emph{DFS.} To decide whether $H_e x=0$, $L_e x\ne0$ has a solution with
$|x|\le W$: a minimum-weight solution is syndrome-minimal; translate it so that it
contains the representative of its lowest orbit; branch on the columns of an odd
syndrome row, forbidding earlier siblings (a partition of the search space); prune
by $\mathrm{depth}+\lceil |s|/w_{\max}\rceil>W$; look up the last column in a hash
table. The symmetry group is computed (translations that are code automorphisms,
map each check's CNOT order to the image check's order, and preserve logical
classes, verified by solving $L\circ T=ML+NH$ with $\mathrm{rank}\,M=k$).
Validation: exact solution counts against unpruned meet-in-the-middle enumeration
on 22 BB72 instances and on $>64$-row direct-sum instances; split-run completeness
(sum of split runs = unsplit run); runs without symmetry for $\code{108,8,10}$
(both sides, $\approx2.5\times10^{10}$ nodes, 0 solutions) and for the four LRC
theorems\cl{19,22}.

\emph{$\code{108,8,10}$ circuit.} Terms indexed $[A_1,A_2,A_3,B_1,B_2,B_3]$ with
$A=(x^3,y,y^2)$, $B=(y^3,x,x^2)$, $(\ell,m)=(9,6)$. $X$ checks: reset at 0, CNOTs at
1--6, measure at 7; term order $[0,1,2,5,3,4]$ on even $y$ rows and
$[2,1,0,5,3,4]$ on odd rows. $Z$ checks: reset at 4, CNOTs at 5,6,7,0,1,2, measure
at 3; term order $[3,4,5,2,1,0]$ on even rows and $[3,4,5,0,1,2]$ on odd rows.
(The $Z$ side applies the $B$ block first; the reversal acts on the $A$ block.)
No data-qubit conflicts, non-interleaved and partitionable.
$\dext^X=\dext^Z=10$ exactly (270 columns per side, 10 orbits of 27;
weight-9 DFS: 0 solutions with and without symmetry; weight 10 attained);
injection-verified weight-10 witness\cl{19}.

\emph{Gross Fig.~12.} 360 merged columns per side (144 of $H_e$-weight 3, 144 of
weight 6, 72 of weight 9); 24 translations ($a+b\equiv0 \bmod 3$) preserve every
check's CNOT order and the logical classes; 15 orbits. Weight-10 DFS: 0 solutions
on the original instance ($\approx2\times10^{10}$ nodes per side), on an
independently built lattice-coordinate instance, and under an order-8 subgroup
(45 orbits, $\approx5\times10^{10}$ nodes per side). Weight-11 solutions realised
as 11 circuit faults for $R=1,2,3$, both bases\cl{10,11}. A run without any
symmetry ($\approx 7\times10^{11}$ nodes) was not performed.

\section{Exact lift test and arbitrary $R$ for the IBM $\code{72,12,6}$ circuit}\label{sm:lift}
Let $F$ be an undetected logical with $|F|=5=\dext$. By the Table-I property each
fault image is zero or one column; cancelling pairs leaves an extended logical $T$
with $|T|\le|F|$. Since no extended logical of weight $\le4$ exists, all five
images are nonzero and distinct and form a weight-5 extended logical. We list all
of them (36 for the $X$-type, 252 for the $Z$-type extended code; meet-in-the-middle
and DFS agree), group the DEM terms by the image of every circuit location
(checked to list all locations: location counts equal those from \texttt{stim}),
and search for one term per column with zero detector sum and nonzero
observable. Result: 0 for $R=1,\dots,10$, both bases; positive control at weight 6
finds solutions\cl{21}.
\emph{Arbitrary $R$.} (1)~The CNOT windows of consecutive cycles are separated.
(2)~If an undetected logical $F$ has an empty round strictly inside its time span,
it splits into two undetected parts whose observables add, one of them a
lighter undetected logical; for $|F|=5=\dext$ this contradicts Prop.~3, so a
weight-5 logical spans at most 5 consecutive rounds. (3)~The noisy $(R+1)$-round
circuit equals the $R$-round circuit with one block inserted, so such a logical
translates into a circuit with $\le7$ rounds, covered by the exact runs.
Hypotheses (1) and (3) and the detector structure were machine-checked; step (3)
is a written argument\cl{21}.

\section{Morphing circuits: construction, single-round instance, fold}\label{sm:fold}
\emph{Construction.} Mid-cycle code $\code{288,12,18}$, $\ell=m=12$,
$A=x^3+y^7+y^2$, $B=y^3+x+x^2$; homomorphism $f_x$; $K=\ker f_x$, $|K|=72$. Each
round: reset the 144 previously measured qubits, apply the 3 layers of the
inverse contraction and the 3 layers of the next contraction (each layer a
perfect matching of 144 CNOTs), measure 144 qubits. Every contracting stabiliser
lands on a single same-basis measured qubit; the end-cycle codes have 72+72
weight-9 generators, rank 66+66, $k=12$, qubit degree 5. Our construction is
gate-identical to an independent one for all 3 homomorphisms $\times$ 2 layer
orders $\times$ 2 bases $\times$ 2 starting rounds. Detectors are derived as the
GF(2) kernel of noiseless samples and split into time-local blocks
$D_t$\cl{1}.

\emph{Instance.} The set of DEM terms equals the set of projected fault
signatures ($T=1..5$, both bases). Every round-$r$ fault touches only $D_r$,
$D_{r+1}$. For $T=1..5$ every round's instance is equivalent to the round-1
instance (time shift plus a translation for even $r$; rank conditions and a
bijection on projected faults). The 72 translations by $K$ are automorphisms of
every layer and induce column permutations preserving undetected logicals; 12
orbits of 72\cl{1}.

\emph{Fold condition (basis-free form).} Let $\mu$ map each round-$r$ fault to its
time-reversed partner in round $r-1$ (checked to be a fault for every round-$r$
fault). Let $Z_r$ be the span of detector functionals that vanish on all faults of
rounds $<r$. If $F$ is undetected with last round $r$ and round-$r$ part $E$, then
$E$ is annihilated by $Z_r$, and $F'=(F\setminus E)\oplus\mu(E)$ is undetected with
the same logical value whenever
\begin{equation}
\Psi(E)=\big[H_{\rm all}(E)+H_{\rm all}(\mu E);\,L(E)+L(\mu E)\big]=0
\quad\text{for all $E$ annihilated by $Z_r$,}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ whenever every row of $\Psi$ lies in the row space of $Z_r$ restricted to
round-$r$ faults. A local version replaces $Z_r$ by the functionals in
$\mathrm{span}(D_r,D_{r+1})$ vanishing on round-$(r-1)$ faults (a subspace, hence
stronger) and depends only on two rounds. Both versions hold for $r=2..T$,
$T=2..5$, both bases, including the noisy final round. Iterating the fold
gives, for every $T$, an undetected logical of no larger weight inside one round,
i.e.\ a solution of the single-round instance. The arbitrary-$T$ step therefore
rests on exact checks for $T\le5$ plus periodicity of the local condition and of
the single-round instance (machine-checked hypotheses plus a written argument).
\emph{Remark.} The per-fault statements in the proof of Prop.~1 of
Ref.~\cite{ShawTerhal2026} (``same detectors'', ``same observables'') do not hold
literally in a general detector basis, and a naive block-wise test fails at the
final round; the condition above is the one the fold needs and it holds\cl{3}.

\emph{Search and witness.} Weight-11 DFS, all 12 orbit cases, both bases:
0 solutions on two equivalent instances (sparse basis: $\approx2.8\times10^{10}$
nodes per basis; record basis, different tree: $\approx2.0\times10^{11}$ nodes per
basis). The end-cycle codes have distance exactly 12 (DFS) and minimum stabiliser
weight exactly 9; injecting a weight-12 end-cycle logical as 12 single-qubit
faults after a reset layer gives 0 detectors and flipped observables for every
admissible round and $T$\cl{1,2}.

the 8 sub-$\dt$ circuits the values were recomputed in $96$ settings (SD6 versus
no idle noise; full $T=4$; $T=5$ with noise only in half-cycles 2--4; $T=5$ with
noise only in one interior half-cycle): identical values throughout. Minimum
witnesses consisting only of CNOT faults in one interior half-cycle were injected
into circuits carrying all $X$- and $Z$-type detectors (16/16 pass)\cl{5,8}.
The 24 circuits with mother code $\code{288,12,18}$ were proven by one pipeline
(single-round DFS plus fold check, all flags verified); three of them
($f_x$ rev with $\dt=10$; $f_y$ std and $f_{xy}$ rev with $\dt=12$) were reproduced
independently on single interior half-cycle instances in both bases, and one also
on the full $T=3$ circuit without folding. The arbitrary-$T$ statement for these
circuits relies on the per-circuit fold computation (code and output flags
audited, not independently re-implemented)\cl{9}.

\emph{Details moved from the main text.} The 6/6/7/7 split is by $b_3$ in the first round

is tight at 7, and all 24 circuits derived from $\code{288,12,18}$ preserve their
end-cycle distance (12 or 10)\cl{6,7,9}.

\section{Gross code with the Fig.~12 orders: lift structure, period-8 family, bulk obstructions}\label{sm:gross12}
This section concerns circuits that keep the CNOT orders of Fig.~12. The frame model
described here is also the one used to design the circuit of SM~\ref{sm:p21}.
Fig.~12 itself: $\dcirc=11$ (main text). Bulk summary (moved from the main text):
the frame model agrees with the exact lift in the bulk but not at the initial time
boundary\cl{18}; every legal deterministic circuit keeping the Fig.~12 orders has a bulk
weight-11 error of ten data faults and one hook for translation-symmetric
non-wrapping circuits of any period, symmetric pipelined circuits with
$P=8,\dots,12$ ($P=12$ by a single encoding), several subgroup-invariant pipelined
families at $P=9,10$, and per-check-free non-wrapping circuits with $P\le16$
(Table~\ref{tab:gross})\cl{16}. In these families the certificate has one form: with
the Fig.~12 orders, the timing conditions that would hide every such hook error
from the detectors are mutually inconsistent.

\emph{Weight-11 logicals.} Exhaustive enumeration with two different DFS programs
gives 4224 weight-11 extended logicals per side, all in free orbits of the
24-element group (176 orbits); 360 of them consist of ten data columns and one
length-2 residual (``10 data + r2''). An exact lift checker augments the circuit
with one observable per row of $H$ and $L$ on the final data readout, so each DEM
term carries its extended-code image; for each extended logical a frontier
dynamic program over the 11 column groups decides liftability exactly. On Fig.~12
all $4224/4224$ lift for $R=1,2,3$ (independently reproduced; the original run
covers $R=1..6$)\cl{13}.

\emph{The benchmark family ($P\le8$).} Rules: one period $P\in[2,8]$ shared by
$X$ and $Z$ checks; per check one reset, six CNOTs at distinct offsets
$0<o_1<\dots<o_6<o_{\rm meas}<P$ and one measurement; each data qubit in at most
one CNOT per time step mod $P$; determinism checked on the circuit. Weight 6
forces $P=8$, $o_k=k$, $o_{\rm meas}=7$; keeping the Fig.~12 orders leaves only
$s_c\in\mathbb{Z}_8$ per check. A non-SAT backtracking search (forward checking,
pigeonhole per data qubit, no symmetry) finds exactly 4 solutions with one start
fixed and 32 $=8\times4$ without; two independent SAT encodings agree. All four
are colour-uniform, deterministic, non-interleaved; one is Fig.~12. The other
lattice isomorphism of Fig.~12 is related by a code relabelling, so the
statement does not depend on that choice. Weight-11 witnesses were injected
through the benchmark's own verification routine: 72/72 (4 circuits $\times$
$R=1..6$ $\times$ 2 bases) fire no detector and flip an observable, with 11
distinct DEM terms\cl{14,15}. Not covered: other CNOT orders; $R\ge7$ for this
finite-$R$ statement.

\emph{Frame model.} A data $X$ error at time $\theta$ is first seen by $Z$ check $c$
(readout times $T_c+nP$) in instance $H_c=\min\{n:T_c+nP>\theta\}$. Three readouts
of a data qubit give three length-$P$ open intervals; pairwise intersection is a
difference constraint on the $H_c$, and by one-dimensional Helly a common time
exists, at which an SD6 fault location is available. A hook (ancilla $X$ after the
fourth CNOT of an $X$ check) fixes its arrival. Feasibility of the difference
constraints (no negative cycle) implies a genuine weight-11 undetected error in the
bulk; this was checked by 75 stim injections. The converse is not used. The model
agrees with the exact lift checker in the bulk but not at the initial time
boundary: on a $P=16$ circuit, 96 logicals blocked in the model lift at $R=2,4,6$
via round-0 detectors\cl{18}.

\emph{Certificates.} Blocking every ``10 data + r2'' logical requires a
negative cycle for each; CP-SAT (path encoding, and independently an unrolled
Bellman--Ford encoding) and a CEGAR SAT loop (lazy determinism clauses; nogoods
from the potentials of liftable logicals, with 24 translated images) give
INFEASIBLE/UNSAT for the families in Table~\ref{tab:gross}\cl{16}.

\emph{Results (moved from the main text).}
For $\code{108,8,10}$ the IBM circuit has $\dcirc\le 8$~\cite{Bravyi2024,ShawTerhal2025},
and while the single-check bound for partitionable non-interleaved circuits does
not exclude 10 for this code, uniform depth-8 tilings are limited to
$\le 9$\cl{24}. A two-colour left-right circuit that
reverses the CNOT order within one term block on checks of one $y$-parity (the
$X$ side on odd rows, the $Z$ side on even rows; depth 8, one ancilla per check,
all checks every round) has $\dcirc=10=d$\cl{19}. To our knowledge this is the
first $\dcirc=10$ circuit for this code and the first circuit distance for a BB
code with $d\ge10$ that comes with a stated, reproducible proof (here BB means
$\ell,m\ge2$; Ref.~\cite{Tripier2026} lists $\dcirc=9$, method unstated, for a
$\code{54,2,10}$ generalized-bicycle code); rigorous circuit distances exist for other qLDPC
families~\cite{ManesClaes,Liang2026,SunCai2026}, and the idea of position-dependent
CNOT orders is established~\cite{TomitaSvore,KishonyFowler,Strikis2026}\cl{20}.

For the IBM $\code{72,12,6}$ circuit~\cite{Bravyi2024}, which is interleaved,
$\dext=5<6$, so Prop.~3 alone does not suffice. The exact lift test shows that
none of the weight-5 extended logicals lifts, for $R=1,\dots,10$, and a
splitting argument extends this to every $R$: $\dcirc=6$ in both bases, settling
IBM's conjecture for this circuit and fault model\cl{21}. Public data of
Ref.~\cite{Webster2026} already contain an exact value 6 for one basis and $R=6$
of a CZ/SI1000 compilation of an IBM-type circuit; our additions are arbitrary
$R$, both bases, the CNOT circuit, and the method\cl{21}.

The same pipeline---colour the checks by a translation subgroup, prune block
orders by single-colour-class witnesses, enumerate legal states, and prove the
survivors by DFS---gives the theorems in the lower half of Table~\ref{tab:bbthm}
\cl{22} and rigorous negative results: every two-colour LRC of the gross code has
$\dcirc\le10$, and every two- or three-class mod-3 LRC of $\code{90,8,10}$ has
$\dcirc\le8$\cl{23}. Apart from $\code{72,12,6}$, these are, to our knowledge,
the first circuit distances for BB codes with $d\ge10$ that come with a stated,
reproducible proof; Ref.~\cite{Tripier2026} reports $\dcirc=9$ for
$\code{70,6,9}$ and $\code{102,22,9}$ BB/GB codes and for a $\code{54,2,10}$ GB
code without describing how these values were obtained\cl{25}.


\emph{Codes and orders.} $\code{72,12,6}$ and $\code{108,8,10}$ as in Ref.~\cite{Bravyi2024}
($A=x^3+y+y^2$, $B=y^3+x+x^2$); coprime $\code{126,12,10}$ ($\ell=7$, $m=9$,
$A=1+\pi+\pi^{58}$, $B=1+\pi^{13}+\pi^{41}$, $\pi=xy$; Ref.~\cite{WangMueller},
Table 2); $\code{120,8,12}$ with $(\ell,m)=(20,3)$, $A=1+x^3y^2+x^7y^2$,
$B=1+x^{16}y+x^9y$ (found in our own code screen; its equivalence to the
$\code{120,8,12}$ code of other works is \unverified); $\code{124,10,10}$ with
$(\ell,m)=(31,2)$, $A=1+x^{27}y+x^{17}$, $B=1+x^{30}y+x^{12}y$ (own screen).
Term orders (indices $[A_1,A_2,A_3,B_1,B_2,B_3]$): $\code{72,12,6}$ two-colour:
as for $\code{108,8,10}$; cp126 uniform: $X$ $[1,0,2,4,3,5]$, $Z$ $[4,3,5,1,0,2]$;
$\code{120}$ and $\code{124}$ uniform: $X$ $[0,1,2,3,4,5]$, $Z$ $[3,4,5,0,1,2]$.
Each theorem: DFS at $W=w-1$ with and without symmetry (0 solutions), DFS at $W=w$
finds solutions, injection-verified weight-$w$ witness for $R=1,2,3$ and by the

All 864 proper two-colour LRC states of $\code{72,12,6}$ have $\dext=6$\cl{22}.

\emph{Negative results.} Gross: for all three index-2 colourings, every two-colour
LRC has a verified weight-10 extended logical, hence $\dcirc\le10$ (Cor.~1;
the template is non-interleaved). $\code{90,8,10}$ ($A=x^9+y+y^2$, $B=1+x^2+x^7$,

circuits (circuits that can be cut in time into alternating $X$-only and

$\code{108,8,10}$ has $\dcirc\le10$ by the single-check argument of Prop.~4 of
Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026}; uniform tilings give $\le8$ ($\code{90}$, any depth) and
$\le9$ ($\code{108}$, depth 8). IBM-type pipelined circuits are pairwise
non-interleaved but not partitionable and are not covered\cl{24}.

\section{Prior work and novelty caveats}\label{sm:novelty}
\begin{itemize}
\item Gross $\dcirc=12$ (search date 2026-10-10; arXiv API on titles/abstracts,
keyword searches, the qldpc-challenge issues): no circuit for the gross code itself
(single- or multi-ancilla, flag or Shor-style) with $\dcirc=12$ shown numerically or
rigorously was found. The open question of Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026} (v2, Sec.~IV.3)
is quoted verbatim in the text; it carries no depth condition, and the paper does
not conjecture impossibility. Its App.~H.2 envisages ``non-staggered, interleaved
variants'' of the depth-8 circuit, i.e.\ the depth-8 question we leave open. Best
previous values for the gross code itself: $\le10$ for the IBM circuit and its 936
depth-7 variants~\cite{Bravyi2024}, $\le10$ in Ref.~\cite{TourDeGross}, 11 for
Fig.~12. The morphing ``12'' of Refs.~\cite{ShawTerhal2025,Wills2026} concerns a
different code;
Ref.~\cite{Nguyen2026} lists lower bounds for interleaved circuits as future work.
Evidence strength medium-high; there is no external reproduction yet\cl{40}.
\item Gross Fig.~12 $=11$: Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026} v2 still states 11 as a
conjecture and suggests exhaustive methods without executing them; no proof of
any gross-circuit $\dcirc\ge11$ was found in the literature checked (search date
2026-10-07)\cl{12}.
\item IBM $\code{72,12,6}$: partial precedent. The public results table of
Ref.~\cite{Webster2026} contains an exact value 6 (two exact solvers) for a
$Z$-basis, $R=6$, CZ+H/SI1000 circuit of IBM type; it is not stated as a result in
the paper and its gate-by-gate identity with the IBM order was not checked. The
GitHub leaderboard~\cite{qldpcchallenge} lists an upper bound 6/6 for $R=6$\cl{21}.
\item $\code{108,8,10}$: literature values are $\le8$; position- or colour-dependent
CNOT orders have precedents~\cite{TomitaSvore,KishonyFowler,Strikis2026}; the
circuit is an instance of the left-right framework of Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026}.
Rigorous circuit-distance results exist for hypergraph-product
codes~\cite{ManesClaes}, layer codes~\cite{SunCai2026}, and as certificates for
amplified codes~\cite{Liang2026}; an algebraic LP framework gives upper
bounds~\cite{Ghosh2026}\cl{20}.
\item Exact circuit distances for BB/GB codes with $d\ge9$ by others:
Ref.~\cite{Tripier2026} lists $\dcirc=9$ (without ``$\le$'') for
$\code{70,6,9}$ BB, $\code{102,22,9}$ GB and $\code{54,2,10}$ GB codes; the method is
not stated anywhere in the paper (v1, the only version; its Tables~9--10 and
Sec.~IX.2 define $\dcirc$ and list the schedules, and ``$\le$'' is used only for
values quoted from other works). We therefore restrict our wording to proofs that
are stated and reproducible, and to $d\ge10$ with $\ell,m\ge2$. Ref.~\cite{Webster2026}
reports that exact solvers completed only the smallest BB circuit of its
benchmark\cl{20,25}.
\item Morphing: no rigorous lower bound on $\dcirc$ for any morphing circuit was
found (search date 2026-10-08); Ref.~\cite{Chao2026} certifies code distances of
related constructions, not circuit distances\cl{4}. A dedicated search
(2026-10-09) found no prior exact circuit distance for any morphing circuit, no
prior evaluation of the 80-circuit family beyond the BP-OSD upper bounds of
Ref.~\cite{ShawTerhal2025}, and no prior result on retimings of the Fig.~12
circuit (whether interleaved circuits can reach 12 is stated as open in
Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026}). Ref.~\cite{Nguyen2026} proposes combining the
non-interleaved lower bound of Ref.~\cite{Strikis2026} with the single-round
reduction of Ref.~\cite{ShawTerhal2026} as future work, without numerical
results\cl{4,29,30}.
\end{itemize}
